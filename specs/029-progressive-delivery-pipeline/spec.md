# Feature Specification: Trunk-Based Progressive Delivery Pipeline

**Feature Branch**: `029-progressive-delivery-pipeline`  
**Created**: 2026-04-26  
**Status**: Draft  
**Input**: User description: "Evolve the existing CI/CD pipeline (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`, `cd.yml`, `drift-detection.yml`) into a trunk-based, progressive-delivery pipeline that lets the team ship small changes from `main` to production in under an hour while satisfying the constitution's life-safety, AU data residency, append-only audit, and PSPF/Privacy Act obligations through automated, evidence-producing guardrails rather than ticket-driven manual approvals. PR gates within 10 minutes (lint, typecheck, unit, contract, integration, simulation, accessibility, benchmark, secret scan, SAST, dependency audit, container scan, Bicep what-if, IaC policy-as-code). CODEOWNERS gates for safety-critical paths. Build-once with SBOM, Cosign signing, SLSA-3 provenance. Staging e2e + load smoke + chaos-lite. Production canary 5/25/50/100 % with SLO burn-rate auto-promotion and auto-rollback. Incident-Mode Change Freeze with 2-person emergency-hotfix lane. Immutable Evidence Pack per production promotion appended to platform audit store. OIDC-only identity, Key Vault references, nightly drift detection."

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-04-26

- Q: How should the SC-001 sub-60-minute lead time target be reconciled with FR-007's per-stage SLO burn-rate window? → A: Use tiered observation windows per canary stage — 10 min at 5%, 15 min at 25%, 30 min at 50%, and a full 60 min at 100% — keeping SC-001 (median lead time to 100% traffic) under 60 minutes for non-safety-critical changes while preserving statistical confidence at higher traffic shares.
- Q: Which gates may be skipped on the emergency-hotfix lane vs remain mandatory? → A: Keep all correctness, safety, supply-chain and policy gates mandatory (lint, typecheck, unit, contract, SAST, secret scan, dependency audit, container image scan, IaC policy / AU-region check, signature/SBOM/provenance verification, Bicep what-if when infra changes, CODEOWNERS for safety-critical paths). Only the long-running gates may be deferred: full integration suite, simulation replay, accessibility full sweep, load smoke, and chaos-lite. Any deferred gate MUST be reconciled within 24 hours of deploy as part of the evidence-pack completion.
- Q: How is "previous known-good revision" defined for auto-rollback? → A: The most recent revision that completed the full canary — i.e., reached 100 % traffic and passed its 60-minute observation window without a fast-burn or slow-burn breach, was not subsequently rolled back, and whose artefact signature, SBOM and provenance still verify. The Artefact entity carries a `last_known_good_at` timestamp set when this condition is first met; rollback selects the artefact with the most recent such timestamp.
- Q: How is authority to invoke the Incident-Mode Change Freeze and approve emergency hotfixes enforced? → A: Authority is granted exclusively via membership in dedicated Entra ID groups (`ama-oncall-sre`, `ama-incident-commander`, `ama-emergency-approver`). Enforcement happens at workflow dispatch and at the audit emitter using OIDC claims that carry group membership; an actor lacking the required claim has the action rejected and the attempt logged as a security event. Group membership changes are themselves recorded in the audit store.
- Q: What retention policy applies to evidence packs and audit-store entries? → A: 7 years for both evidence packs and audit-store entries, with legal hold (immutability policy denying modification or deletion) enabled for the duration. Retention is uniform across deployment, incident, and operator audit events to preserve a single provenance chain. The 7-year window is sized to cover Privacy Act §15B record-keeping for serious-harm incidents, PSPF protective-marking retention, and state Emergency Management Act review periods.
- Q: Does the AU-region constraint apply to Microsoft Foundry model deployments? → A: No. Microsoft Foundry model deployments are explicitly carved out of the AU-region requirement because of current model availability and quota restrictions in Australian regions. All other Azure resources (compute, data, storage, telemetry, audit store, evidence store, networking) MUST remain in `australiaeast` or `australiasoutheast`. Foundry deployments outside AU MUST be limited to model-hosting endpoints only — no regulated platform data may persist outside AU regions — and prompts/responses involving personal information or PSPF-marked content MUST be subject to data-minimisation controls at the gateway. The carve-out and its scope are recorded in IaC policy as an explicit, named exception so that auditors can retrieve the list of permitted non-AU Foundry resources.
- Q: How are the three environments (`dev`, `staging`, `production`) isolated at the Azure platform level? → A: The pipeline MUST support **either** of two equally valid Azure isolation topologies, selectable per deployment of this solution and recorded in `infra/IDENTITY.md`:
  - **Topology A — Subscription-per-environment**: three Azure subscriptions (one each for `dev`, `staging`, `production`), each containing a resource group `rg-ama-{env}-{region}` (e.g., `rg-ama-dev-australiaeast`). Azure Policy assignments (AU-region, posture matrix, deny-public-endpoint for non-`dev`) are scoped at the **subscription** level. This is the recommended target topology for production-grade tenants with multiple subscriptions available. *(Note: the `subscriptionTopology` Bicep parameter introduced by T019a defaults to `'B'` for backwards compatibility with the personal-MCAP build path; tenants targeting Topology A MUST override the parameter explicitly per environment in `infra/parameters/{dev,staging,production}.bicepparam`.)*
  - **Topology B — Single-subscription, RG-per-environment**: one Azure subscription with three resource groups `rg-ama-dev-{region}`, `rg-ama-staging-{region}`, `rg-ama-prod-{region}`. Azure Policy assignments are scoped at the **resource-group** level. This is the supported topology for tenants with only one subscription available (e.g., personal MCAP / sandbox subscriptions).
  In both topologies, cross-environment role assignments are denied by IaC policy gate (FR-029) and by an Azure Policy that blocks role-assignment scopes that traverse environment boundaries (subscription or RG, whichever applies). The chosen topology MUST be uniform across all three environments (no mixing). Subscription-vs-RG isolation choice is the only platform-level isolation variable; tenant-level or management-group separation is out of scope for this feature.
- Q: What is the topology of the `dev` environment — single shared, ephemeral per-PR, or per-developer? → A: Single shared `dev` environment used by all developers. Per-developer separation is achieved only via (a) developer-specific Key Vault entries holding short-lived API keys (FR-026, FR-030) and (b) naming-prefix conventions on developer-created throwaway test data. Ephemeral per-PR review environments and per-developer persistent environments are explicitly out of scope for this feature; integration validation prior to `staging` continues to rely on the `staging` environment itself.
- Q: What IP allow-list scope applies to public endpoints in `dev`? → A: Open (`0.0.0.0/0`) — public endpoints in `dev` are reachable from the public internet. Compensating controls are: (a) `dev` contains no real personal information or PSPF-marked content (existing assumption), (b) authentication is mandatory on every data-plane endpoint (no anonymous access), (c) `dev` API keys are short-lived (default 24-hour rotation per FR-030) and dev-scoped, (d) every `dev` data-plane endpoint MUST emit access logs to the audit/observability store, and (e) any anomalous access volume triggers a security event for review. The IP allow-list as a primary control is explicitly rejected in favour of identity-and-rotation as the primary control, simplifying remote/hybrid developer onboarding.
- Q: What is the canonical just-in-time access pattern for developers needing to reach `staging` or `production` backends? → A: Azure Bastion plus a hardened jump-host VM in each `staging`/`production` VNet. Developers connect via the Bastion browser/SSH tunnel, then reach private-endpoint-fronted backends from inside the VNet. RBAC for the jump-host and target services is granted just-in-time via Microsoft Entra Privileged Identity Management (PIM) with time-bounded role activation (default 1 hour, max 8 hours), Managed-Identity-only authentication to data-plane services (per FR-027), and full session/audit logging to the platform observability and audit stores. Direct point-to-site VPN, public endpoints, and shared keys remain prohibited in `staging`/`production`.
- Q: Where does the posture matrix live and what evaluates it? → A: A single canonical file at `infra/policy/posture-matrix.yaml`, version-controlled, CODEOWNERS-gated to the security and compliance personas. It is the sole source of truth and is consumed by (a) the OPA/conftest IaC policy gate at PR time, (b) a generator that emits the equivalent runtime Azure Policy assignments per environment resource group, and (c) the post-deploy posture-verification step (FR-031). Any change to a posture rule occurs only via PR to this file; ad-hoc Azure Policy edits in the portal are prohibited and surfaced by drift detection (FR-019).
- Q: How are `dev` API keys issued and rotated? → A: Pipeline-managed self-service via a dedicated `gh workflow dispatch` workflow (`dev-key-issue.yml`) that runs under the pipeline's federated OIDC workload identity. The workflow (a) authenticates the requesting developer via their Entra ID identity (`workflow_dispatch` actor claim), (b) mints or rotates a scoped data-plane key for the requested `dev` service via the Azure SDK, (c) writes the key to the developer-specific Key Vault path `kv-ama-dev/secrets/devkey-{alias}-{service}` with a 24-hour expiry, (d) revokes any prior key for that `{alias, service}` pair, and (e) emits an append-only audit-store entry (`actor`, `timestamp`, `action=devkey.issue|rotate`, `service`, `expiry`, `outcome`). No human or service principal holds standing rights to mint `dev` keys outside this workflow; FR-016 (no long-lived cloud credentials) is preserved end-to-end.
- Q: How does the IaC policy gate detect cross-environment references (FR-029)? → A: Two layers: (1) at PR time, a static check on the rendered Bicep what-if output uses regex/structural matching against the canonical environment naming token (`-dev-`, `-staging-`, `-prod-`) and the target environment's resource group name to fail the PR if any resource ID, role-assignment scope, or Key Vault reference traverses environments; (2) at deploy time, a pre-flight Azure Resource Graph query asserts that every resource ID referenced by the deployment lives in the target environment's resource group, blocking the deploy on any traversal. Both layers MUST pass; the deploy-time layer catches dynamic / parameter-driven references that the static layer cannot.
- Q: What is the inventory scope of the post-deploy posture verification (FR-031)? → A: Full sweep of every posture-covered resource in the target environment's resource group, on every deploy to every environment. The verifier issues an Azure Resource Graph query against the RG, joins it to the posture matrix (`infra/policy/posture-matrix.yaml`), and reports per-resource compliance. Delta-only verification is explicitly rejected because it would let pre-existing out-of-band drift persist undetected between deploys. Verification failure for `staging` or `production` MUST fail the deploy and trigger rollback per FR-008; verification failure for `dev` MUST raise a tracked issue but does not block the deploy.
- Q: What is the approval workflow and lifetime for posture-matrix exceptions (FR-033)? → A: Exceptions are added via PR to `infra/policy/posture-exceptions.yaml`, require dual approval from BOTH the security CODEOWNER and the compliance CODEOWNER, carry a mandatory `expires_at` field with a maximum lifetime of 90 days from issue date, are auto-removed by a scheduled workflow on expiry (after which the gate enforces the underlying posture rule), and any renewal requires a fresh dual-approver PR (renewals are not silent extensions). Each exception entry MUST include: name, justification, compensating controls, owning team, `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `dual_approvers` (security + compliance GitHub handles), and an audit-store pointer. The quarterly review (FR-033) becomes a backstop check that all entries have valid approvals and unexpired dates, not the primary lifetime control.
- Q: How is "anomalous access volume" detected on dev data-plane endpoints (FR-026)? → A: The platform MUST rely on Microsoft Defender for Cloud (data-plane threat protection on Cosmos DB, Storage, Key Vault, AKS, etc.) plus Microsoft Entra ID Protection (risky sign-in / risky user signals against Entra IDs used for dev data-plane access) as the canonical anomaly detectors for the dev environment. Both services MUST be enabled on the dev subscription; their alerts MUST be routed to the same on-call security channel used by `staging`/`production` and persisted to the central audit store. No bespoke threshold logic is introduced in this feature; dev-specific tuning (e.g., suppression rules) is done inside the Defender / Entra ID Protection consoles by the security CODEOWNER. This trades fine-grained custom thresholds for a maintained, vendor-managed detection surface and avoids duplicating ML/heuristic work.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 — Sub-Hour Lead Time via Trunk-Based Auto-Promotion (Priority: P1)

As a software developer working on a non-safety-critical change, I want my pull request to flow from `main` through staging into 100 % of production traffic in under one hour without filing a change ticket or attending an approval meeting, so that small, frequently-shipped improvements compound into a more reliable platform without bottlenecking on human review.

**Why this priority**: Lead time is the central promise of Option A. Every other capability in this feature exists to make this promise *safe*. Without sub-hour auto-promotion, the pipeline is functionally identical to the current `017-cd-pipeline` and provides no new value.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by merging a low-risk change (e.g., a copy update on a non-safety-critical page) to `main` and observing that, with no human intervention, the change reaches 100 % of production traffic within 60 minutes and the lead-time metric is recorded.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a pull request that touches only non-safety-critical files passes all PR gates, **When** the author merges to `main`, **Then** the change reaches 100 % production traffic in under 60 minutes with no manual approval steps.
2. **Given** auto-promotion is in progress at 5 % traffic, **When** the stage-specific SLO burn-rate window (10 min at 5 %, 15 min at 25 %, 30 min at 50 %) stays healthy, **Then** the canary advances to the next stage automatically, reaching 100 % traffic in under 60 minutes for non-safety-critical changes.
3. **Given** a change is being auto-promoted, **When** any human pauses, cancels, or rolls forward via a single workflow control, **Then** the action is recorded in the audit trail with actor, timestamp, and rationale.
4. **Given** the lead-time metric is published, **When** a stakeholder reviews the deployment dashboard, **Then** they can see lead time per change, change failure rate, and MTTR for the trailing 30 days.

### User Story 2 — Build-Once Signed Artefact with Verified Provenance (Priority: P1)

As a security engineer, I want every container image and infrastructure bundle to be built exactly once on merge, signed, and accompanied by a software bill of materials and build provenance, with the same digest flowing unchanged through staging and production, so that what is verified in test is bit-for-bit what runs in production and the supply chain is auditable end-to-end.

**Why this priority**: A progressive delivery pipeline that re-builds artefacts per environment cannot guarantee that the canary represents the staging tests. Build-once-promote-the-digest is the foundation that makes every later gate trustworthy and is a prerequisite for PSPF supply-chain expectations.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by inspecting two consecutive deployments of the same merge — the digest, signature, SBOM hash, and provenance attestation must be identical across staging and all production canary stages.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a change merges to `main`, **When** the build job completes, **Then** each service has exactly one signed image digest, one SBOM, and one provenance attestation associated with that merge.
2. **Given** an image is being deployed, **When** the deploy step runs in any environment, **Then** the deploy fails closed if the image signature, SBOM, or provenance cannot be verified against the expected build identity.
3. **Given** an artefact has been promoted to production, **When** a reviewer queries the audit trail by image digest, **Then** they can retrieve the originating commit, build run, SBOM, scan reports, and signing identity.
4. **Given** an attempt is made to deploy an image not produced by the pipeline (e.g., a manually tagged image), **When** the deploy step runs, **Then** the deploy is rejected and the attempt is logged as a security event.

### User Story 3 — Comprehensive PR Quality Gates Within a 10-Minute Budget (Priority: P1)

As a principal engineer, I want every pull request to be evaluated against the full constitutional quality bar — correctness, safety, accessibility, security, supply chain, and infrastructure policy — within a 10-minute total wall-clock budget, so that contributors get fast, trustworthy feedback and merging green is a reliable signal that the change is safe to auto-promote.

**Why this priority**: Trunk-based, auto-promoted delivery is only as safe as the gates that decide a change is "green." If gates are slow, contributors batch changes (defeating trunk-based delivery); if gates are weak, defects reach production faster. Gate breadth and speed are equally non-negotiable.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by submitting representative pull requests (a Python change, a TypeScript change, a Bicep change, a change to a safety-critical path, and a change with a deliberately introduced vulnerability) and confirming each gate runs, that wall-clock time from PR open to gate completion is under 10 minutes for the median PR, and that the deliberately bad changes are blocked.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a pull request is opened, **When** the PR pipeline runs, **Then** it executes lint, typecheck, unit tests, contract tests, integration tests, simulation tests, accessibility audit, benchmark, secret scan, SAST, dependency audit, container scan, Bicep what-if, and IaC policy checks, and reports the result of each as an individual status check.
2. **Given** the PR pipeline runs, **When** wall-clock time is measured from "checks queued" to "all checks complete," **Then** the median is under 10 minutes for changes that do not trigger a full simulation or load suite.
3. **Given** a pull request introduces a known vulnerable dependency, an embedded secret, an axe-core accessibility violation, or a Bicep resource targeting a non-Australian region (other than a permitted Microsoft Foundry model-hosting endpoint), **When** the PR pipeline runs, **Then** the PR is blocked from merging with an actionable, link-rich failure report.
4. **Given** a pull request modifies a safety-critical path (alerts, evacuation, recommendations, content-safety, or infrastructure), **When** the PR is opened, **Then** the security and compliance personas are required reviewers via CODEOWNERS, and the PR cannot merge without their approval even if all automated gates pass.

### User Story 4 — SLO Burn-Rate Auto-Promotion and Auto-Rollback (Priority: P2)

As a site reliability engineer, I want production rollout to advance or roll back automatically based on a one-hour SLO burn-rate window covering availability, latency, dashboard load, signal freshness, and recommendation correctness, so that bad changes are detected and reverted within minutes — faster than any human pager response — while good changes proceed without manual gating.

**Why this priority**: Auto-promotion without auto-rollback shifts risk from humans to luck. Burn-rate-driven promotion is the safety mechanism that makes US1 acceptable to operate.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by deploying a deliberate latency-injecting change to production canary at 5 %, observing that fast-burn alerts trigger within minutes, that traffic is shifted back to the previous known-good revision automatically, and that the rollback completes within 5 minutes of the initial breach.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a canary is at any pre-100 % stage, **When** the stage-specific burn-rate window (10 min at 5 %, 15 min at 25 %, 30 min at 50 %) stays healthy across all SLOs (availability, p95 API latency, p95 dashboard load, signal freshness, recommendation correctness), **Then** traffic advances to the next stage; at 100 % a full 60-minute observation window applies before the change is declared stable.
2. **Given** a canary is at any traffic share, **When** a fast-burn breach occurs (5 % of error budget consumed in 1 hour), **Then** traffic is reverted to the previous known-good revision within 5 minutes and a rollback record is appended to the audit trail.
3. **Given** a canary is at any traffic share, **When** a slow-burn breach occurs (10 % of error budget consumed in 6 hours), **Then** auto-promotion pauses, a tracked issue is opened with the SLO context, and traffic is held at the current share without rollback.
4. **Given** the web tier (App Service) is being promoted, **When** the canary advances, **Then** the equivalent slot-swap canary mechanism applies the same percentage stages and the same burn-rate criteria.
5. **Given** a rollback is triggered, **When** the previous known-good revision also fails health checks, **Then** the pipeline raises a critical alert requiring human intervention and stops further automated rollback attempts.

### User Story 5 — Immutable Evidence Pack and Unified Audit Trail (Priority: P2)

As a compliance auditor, I want every production promotion to produce a signed, immutable evidence pack containing all gate results, scans, signatures, infrastructure diffs, and approver identities, written to an Australian-region store with legal hold and mirrored as a structured entry in the platform's append-only audit store, so that every production change can be defended against Privacy Act, NDB, PSPF, and state Emergency Management Act audits without manual evidence collection.

**Why this priority**: The constitution mandates an append-only audit trail and AU data residency. Without an automated evidence pack, compliance becomes a manual scramble per regulator request and the pipeline cannot demonstrate "automation-as-auditor."

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by triggering a production promotion and confirming that within 5 minutes of completion an evidence pack exists in the AU-region store with legal hold, that its contents are individually retrievable, that its hash is recorded in the platform audit store using the same actor/timestamp/action/rationale/outcome schema as incident events, and that attempts to modify or delete the pack are denied.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a production promotion completes (success or failure), **When** the evidence assembly step runs, **Then** an evidence pack is written to an AU-region object store with legal hold within 5 minutes, containing: test results, SBOM, image signature, vulnerability scan reports, Bicep what-if diff, accessibility audit report, IaC policy decisions, deploying actor, signed approval log (where applicable), and linked spec / FR / requirement IDs.
2. **Given** an evidence pack is written, **When** the audit emitter runs, **Then** an entry is appended to the platform audit store using the schema `{actor, timestamp, action, rationale, outcome, evidence_uri, digest}` so deploys, incident decisions, and operator actions share a single provenance chain.
3. **Given** an attempt is made to modify or delete an evidence pack within its retention window, **When** the storage system processes the request, **Then** the request is denied and the attempt is logged as a security event.
4. **Given** an auditor opens an incident review, **When** they query the audit store by incident time window, **Then** they can correlate operator actions, recommendation decisions, and any deployments that occurred in the same window via the unified schema.

### User Story 6 — Incident-Mode Change Freeze with Emergency-Hotfix Lane (Priority: P2)

As an incident commander or on-call SRE, I want a single command that freezes all non-emergency production deployments for the duration of an active incident, while still permitting clearly-labelled emergency hotfixes through a 2-person rule with post-hoc evidence reconciliation, so that the platform stays stable during disasters without removing the team's ability to fix the very bug that is amplifying the incident.

**Why this priority**: A trunk-based pipeline that auto-deploys during a SEV-1 actively endangers life-safety operations. The freeze-and-break-glass mechanism is what makes auto-promotion compatible with Constitution Principle I (Primacy of Life).

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by activating the change freeze, attempting a normal merge (must be queued), then submitting a labelled emergency hotfix with two recorded approvers (must deploy), and confirming that the emergency hotfix produces a complete evidence pack within 24 hours after the freeze is lifted.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a member of the `ama-oncall-sre` or `ama-incident-commander` Entra ID group invokes the freeze control, **When** the freeze activates, **Then** all in-flight non-emergency production promotions pause at their current traffic share and all subsequent merges queue without deploying to production.
2. **Given** the change freeze is active, **When** a pull request labelled `emergency-hotfix` is opened and approved by two distinct authorised humans, **Then** the change may proceed to production with the *emergency gate set*: all correctness, safety, supply-chain and policy gates remain mandatory (lint, typecheck, unit, contract, SAST, secret scan, dependency audit, container scan, IaC policy / AU-region check, signature/SBOM/provenance verification, Bicep what-if when infra is touched, CODEOWNERS for safety-critical paths); only long-running gates may be deferred (full integration suite, simulation replay, accessibility full sweep, load smoke, chaos-lite).
3. **Given** an emergency hotfix is deployed under freeze, **When** 24 hours pass after deploy, **Then** a complete evidence pack must exist for that deploy and a follow-up issue must be open if any normally-required gate was deferred.
4. **Given** the incident is resolved, **When** the freeze is lifted, **Then** queued merges resume promotion in order and the freeze activation/deactivation is recorded in the audit trail with actor and rationale.

### User Story 7 — Continuous Drift Detection and OIDC-Only Identity (Priority: P3)

As a platform operator, I want the pipeline's identity to be exclusively GitHub OIDC federated to Entra ID workload identities — with no static cloud secrets anywhere — and I want a nightly comparison of live Azure state against the Bicep desired state that opens issues on divergence, so that the platform's runtime configuration cannot silently drift away from its declared, reviewed state and no leaked credential can deploy on our behalf.

**Why this priority**: This capability already partially exists (`drift-detection.yml`, partial OIDC). The story formalises the "no exceptions" posture and binds drift findings into the same evidence/audit chain, but it is additive rather than foundational.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by (a) auditing the GitHub repository and pipeline configuration to confirm no long-lived cloud secrets exist, and (b) manually changing a property of an Azure resource outside IaC and confirming that the next nightly run opens a labelled issue identifying the specific property and resource.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the pipeline runs any cloud-touching step, **When** authentication occurs, **Then** it uses GitHub OIDC federated to an Entra ID workload identity scoped to the minimum permissions required for that step.
2. **Given** any environment variable, container configuration, or pipeline input is evaluated, **When** the secret-hygiene lint runs, **Then** the build fails closed if any plain secret is detected outside an Azure Key Vault reference.
3. **Given** the nightly drift-detection workflow runs, **When** live Azure state diverges from Bicep desired state, **Then** an issue with the label `infrastructure-drift` is opened detailing each drifted resource and property, and the finding is appended to the audit store.
4. **Given** drift has been identified, **When** the next merge that touches the affected resource is processed, **Then** the pipeline surfaces the open drift issue as additional context on the PR.

### User Story 8 — Environment-Tiered Infrastructure Posture (dev / staging / production) (Priority: P2)

As a platform engineer, I want the three deployment environments — `dev`, `staging`, and `production` — to each have an explicit, declared infrastructure posture (network exposure, identity, secret handling) so that developers retain a fast local loop in `dev` while `staging` and `production` enforce the strict private-network and Managed-Identity-only baseline that a life-safety regulated platform requires.

**Why this priority**: Trunk-based progressive delivery is only credible if the environment it promotes into is *defensible*. A uniform "everything private, no keys" policy across all environments would slow developer iteration to a crawl (developers cannot reach Cosmos DB from a laptop without a private link); a uniform "anything goes" policy would expose `production`. Tiered posture is the deliberate trade-off that lets the team move fast in `dev` and lock down the regulated estate in `staging`/`production`.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by deploying the same Bicep stack to `dev`, `staging`, and `production` with the appropriate environment parameter and confirming that (a) `dev` data-plane resources expose a public endpoint and accept a scoped, time-bounded API key for a developer's local session, (b) `staging` and `production` resources expose only private endpoints and accept only Managed Identity / OIDC-federated workload identity authentication, and (c) `dev` resources are tagged and named in a way that prevents them being mistaken for, or referenced by, `staging` / `production`.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an infrastructure deployment targets `dev`, **When** the IaC executes, **Then** designated data-plane services (e.g., Cosmos DB, Storage, Key Vault, Service Bus, Event Grid) MAY expose a public endpoint reachable from the public internet (no IP allow-list required), MAY have shared-key (API key) authentication enabled alongside Managed Identity, and MUST be tagged `environment=dev`. Anonymous access MUST remain disabled, and every endpoint MUST emit access logs.
2. **Given** an infrastructure deployment targets `staging` or `production`, **When** the IaC executes, **Then** all data-plane services MUST be deployed behind a private endpoint with public network access disabled, and MUST have shared-key / API-key / connection-string authentication disabled where the service supports it (Managed Identity / Entra ID is the only permitted authentication mode).
3. **Given** a developer is iterating locally against a `dev` backend, **When** they authenticate to a `dev` Cosmos DB or Storage account, **Then** they MAY use either a scoped, time-bounded API key resolved from a developer-specific Key Vault entry OR Managed Identity via Azure CLI login — both paths MUST work without code changes.
4. **Given** a Bicep PR introduces a public endpoint, an enabled shared-key, or a connection-string secret to a `staging` or `production` parameter file, **When** the PR's IaC policy gate runs, **Then** the PR is blocked with a posture-violation report identifying the resource, the offending property, and the remediation.
5. **Given** a `dev` API key exists, **When** secret-rotation policy runs, **Then** the key is re-issued on a defined schedule (default 24 hours) and the old key is revoked; expired keys MUST NOT remain valid.
6. **Given** any environment, **When** an actor attempts to read a `dev` API key from outside the developer-specific Key Vault scope, **Then** access is denied and the attempt is logged as a security event.

### User Story 9 — Pipeline-Enforced Posture Boundaries (Priority: P2)

As a security engineer, I want the pull-request and deployment gates to enforce the dev / staging / production posture matrix mechanically, so that no developer can promote a `dev`-grade configuration into `staging` or `production` by accident or in haste.

**Why this priority**: A posture matrix that exists only as documentation is a posture matrix that drifts. Without machine-enforced boundaries, the convenience of `dev` will leak into the regulated environments — exactly the failure mode the staging / production baseline is designed to prevent.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by submitting representative pull requests that (a) try to enable shared-key auth on a `staging` Cosmos DB, (b) try to add a public network rule to a `production` Storage account, (c) try to embed a connection string in a `production` parameter file, and (d) try to grant a `dev`-scoped Key Vault role to a `production` workload identity — all four must be blocked with a clear failure report citing the offending posture rule.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a PR modifies infrastructure for `staging` or `production`, **When** the IaC policy gate runs, **Then** it evaluates the posture rules below and fails closed if any are violated:
   - public network access enabled on a data-plane resource;
   - shared-key / local-auth enabled where the service supports disabling it;
   - any connection-string, key, or password embedded as a literal in the parameter file or template;
   - any role assignment that grants a `dev`-scoped identity access to a `staging`/`production` resource (or vice versa).
2. **Given** a PR introduces a new Azure resource type, **When** the IaC policy gate evaluates it against the posture matrix, **Then** if no posture rule exists for that resource type the PR is blocked pending a compliance-persona review that adds an explicit rule to the matrix.
3. **Given** a runtime drift introduces a non-conformant posture (e.g., shared-key re-enabled on `production` Cosmos DB out-of-band), **When** the nightly drift-detection workflow runs, **Then** the drift is opened as a high-severity `posture-violation` issue and the audit store records the deviation with actor (where attributable) and timestamp.
4. **Given** the pipeline deploys to any environment, **When** the post-deploy verification runs, **Then** it asserts the actual posture (network exposure, identity, secret presence) matches the declared posture for that environment and fails the deployment if it does not.
5. **Given** an emergency-hotfix deploy targets `production`, **When** the emergency gate set runs, **Then** the posture-matrix gate is *non-skippable* — it remains mandatory regardless of emergency status.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when SLO measurements themselves are unavailable (telemetry pipeline degraded) at the moment a canary would otherwise advance? The pipeline must hold the current traffic share, raise a tracked issue, and require manual confirmation to advance — never advance on missing data.
- What happens when an emergency hotfix is required while the SLO measurement system is itself broken? The 2-person rule still applies; the evidence pack records "SLO unavailable" and the rollback target is the previous known-good revision rather than burn-rate-driven.
- What happens when two simultaneous merges produce two artefacts and the canary is mid-rollout? The pipeline serialises promotions per service; the second artefact queues and only begins its own canary after the first reaches 100 % or rolls back.
- What happens when the Australian-region evidence store is unavailable at promotion time? The promotion fails closed; partial evidence is written to a local immutable buffer and replayed when the store recovers, with the gap recorded in the audit trail.
- What happens when CODEOWNERS approvers are unavailable during a normal business request (not an active incident)? The PR queues; the pipeline does not auto-promote without the required review, and the freeze/break-glass lane is reserved for genuine incidents.
- What happens when the SBOM, signature, or provenance of an otherwise-promoted artefact later fails re-verification (e.g., key rotation issue)? The artefact is treated as untrusted, no further promotions occur for it, and a tracked issue is opened.
- What happens when accessibility, simulation, or load-smoke gates flake intermittently? Flakes are not retried automatically; the gate fails the run, an issue is opened with the failure trace, and the contributor must rebase or rerun explicitly.
- What happens when the change freeze is invoked while a canary is at 50 %? Traffic is held at 50 %; the canary neither advances nor rolls back unless an SLO breach or explicit operator action occurs.
- What happens when a deploy targets a region accidentally outside `australiaeast` / `australiasoutheast`? The IaC policy gate denies the change at PR time; if it somehow reaches deploy, Azure Policy denies the resource and the deploy fails closed with the specific resource and policy ID. The sole exception is Microsoft Foundry model-hosting endpoints declared on the named carve-out list; those resources are permitted by both the gate and Azure Policy, but every other resource type targeting a non-AU region is denied.
- What happens when a rollback would itself require a fresh build (e.g., the previous image was garbage-collected)? Image retention policy must guarantee N previous known-good digests are retained; if not present, manual intervention is escalated.
- What happens when a developer needs to test a fix against a `staging`-grade backend from their laptop? They MUST connect via Azure Bastion to a hardened jump-host VM inside the `staging` VNet, with role activation granted just-in-time through Microsoft Entra PIM (default 1-hour, max 8-hour activation) and Managed-Identity-only authentication to backends from the jump-host. Shared keys, point-to-site VPN, and public endpoints are NEVER opened on `staging` or `production` for developer convenience.
- What happens when a `dev` API key is accidentally committed to source? The PR-gate secret scan blocks the merge; if the key has reached `main`, the rotation policy revokes it on next cycle and a security-event audit entry records the leak and remediation.
- What happens when a new Azure resource type is introduced for which no posture rule exists yet? The IaC policy gate blocks the PR; the contributor must add an explicit rule to the posture matrix, reviewed by the compliance persona, before the resource may be deployed to any environment.
- What happens when a `dev` resource is referenced (e.g., by name or resource ID) from a `staging` or `production` template? The IaC policy gate denies cross-environment references; environment isolation is non-negotiable.
- What happens when a service does not support disabling shared-key auth (legacy or vendor constraint)? The posture matrix MUST record the explicit, named exception with justification and compensating controls (e.g., the key is held only in a Key Vault accessible to a single Managed Identity), and the exception is reviewed quarterly by the compliance persona.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: System MUST run a complete pull-request gate set on every PR including lint, typecheck, unit, contract, integration, simulation (signal → incident → recommendation → alert), accessibility audit, benchmark, secret scan, SAST, dependency vulnerability audit, container image scan, Bicep what-if (when infrastructure changes), and infrastructure policy-as-code evaluation, reporting each as an independent status check.
- **FR-002**: System MUST complete the median pull-request gate set in under 10 minutes wall-clock time, achieved through job sharding, parallelism, and caching.
- **FR-003**: System MUST enforce CODEOWNERS-driven required reviews on changes that touch safety-critical paths (alerting, evacuation, recommendation, content-safety, infrastructure) such that no automated gate set alone can authorise such a merge.
- **FR-004**: System MUST build each deployable artefact (service container image, infrastructure bundle) exactly once per merge to `main`, generate a software bill of materials, sign the image, and emit a build provenance attestation at SLSA Level 3 or equivalent.
- **FR-005**: System MUST promote the same artefact digest unchanged from staging into production canary stages and refuse to deploy any artefact whose signature, SBOM, or provenance cannot be verified at deploy time.
- **FR-006**: System MUST automatically deploy successful merges to a staging environment and execute the staging gate set: full end-to-end suite, a five-minute load smoke at the platform's documented burst targets, and a curated chaos-lite scenario covering at least one external feed outage and one data-store partition failure.
- **FR-007**: System MUST promote production deployments through a four-stage canary at 5 %, 25 %, 50 %, and 100 % traffic shares, advancing only when stage-specific SLO burn-rate observation windows remain healthy across availability, p95 API latency, p95 dashboard load, signal freshness, and recommendation correctness. Default observation windows are 10 minutes at 5 %, 15 minutes at 25 %, 30 minutes at 50 %, and 60 minutes at 100 %; each window MUST also satisfy a minimum-sample threshold (configurable per service) before its outcome is considered valid.
- **FR-008**: System MUST automatically revert traffic to the previous *known-good* revision within 5 minutes of detecting a fast-burn breach (5 % of error budget consumed in 1 hour) at any canary stage and record the rollback in the audit trail. A *known-good revision* is defined as the most recent revision that (a) reached 100 % production traffic, (b) passed its 60-minute 100 %-stage observation window without a fast-burn or slow-burn breach, (c) has not subsequently been rolled back, and (d) whose artefact signature, SBOM, and provenance still verify at rollback time.
- **FR-009**: System MUST pause promotion (without rolling back) and open a tracked issue when a slow-burn breach (10 % of error budget consumed in 6 hours) is detected.
- **FR-010**: System MUST apply the equivalent slot-swap canary mechanism to the web (App Service) tier, with the same traffic stages and burn-rate criteria as Container Apps services.
- **FR-011**: System MUST provide an Incident-Mode Change Freeze toggle that is invocable only by actors whose OIDC claims include membership in the `ama-oncall-sre` or `ama-incident-commander` Entra ID group. Invocation by any other actor MUST be rejected and logged as a security event. While the freeze is active, all non-emergency production promotions pause and subsequent merges queue.
- **FR-011a**: System MUST require that both approvers of an `emergency-hotfix` change hold OIDC claims for membership in the `ama-emergency-approver` Entra ID group, that the two approvers are distinct identities, and that neither approver is the change author. Membership changes to any of these groups MUST themselves be recorded in the audit store.
- **FR-012**: System MUST support an emergency-hotfix lane that requires two distinct authorised human approvers per change and that runs the *emergency gate set*: all correctness, safety, supply-chain and policy gates remain mandatory (lint, typecheck, unit, contract, SAST, secret scan, dependency audit, container image scan, IaC policy and AU-region enforcement, signature / SBOM / provenance verification, Bicep what-if when infrastructure is touched, and CODEOWNERS reviews for safety-critical paths). Only long-running gates may be deferred: full integration suite, simulation replay, accessibility full sweep, load smoke, and chaos-lite. Any deferred gate MUST be executed and reconciled into the evidence pack within 24 hours of deploy; failure to reconcile MUST raise a tracked critical issue and block further emergency deploys until resolved.
- **FR-013**: System MUST assemble an immutable evidence pack within 5 minutes of every production promotion (success or failure) containing all gate results, SBOM, signatures, scan reports, Bicep what-if diff, accessibility report, IaC policy decisions, deploying actor, approval log, and linked specification identifiers.
- **FR-014**: System MUST write evidence packs to an Australian-region object store with a 7-year retention policy and legal-hold (immutability) protections that deny modification or deletion within the retention window. Audit-store entries related to deployment, incident, and operator events MUST be retained under the same 7-year append-only policy to preserve a single provenance chain.
- **FR-015**: System MUST emit a structured entry to the platform's append-only audit store for every deployment-related event (gate decision, promotion, rollback, freeze activation, evidence-pack write) using a schema compatible with operator and incident audit events (`actor`, `timestamp`, `action`, `rationale`, `outcome`, plus deployment-specific fields).
- **FR-016**: System MUST authenticate to all cloud services using GitHub OIDC federated to Entra ID workload identities. No long-lived cloud credentials may exist in pipeline configuration, repository secrets, or runners.
- **FR-017**: System MUST resolve all application secrets through Azure Key Vault references and MUST fail any deploy whose configuration contains a plain secret outside of a Key Vault reference.
- **FR-018**: System MUST deny any infrastructure change that targets a region other than `australiaeast` or `australiasoutheast`, both at policy-as-code evaluation in the PR gate and at runtime via Azure Policy. Microsoft Foundry model-hosting endpoints are the sole permitted exception, and only when declared in a named, version-controlled carve-out list reviewed by the security and compliance personas. The carve-out applies exclusively to model-hosting resources; no regulated platform data (incidents, alerts, recommendations, audit, evidence, telemetry, secrets) may persist outside AU regions, and prompts or responses traversing non-AU Foundry endpoints MUST be governed by data-minimisation controls at the platform's model gateway.
- **FR-019**: System MUST run a nightly drift-detection comparison between live Azure state and the Bicep desired state and open a labelled issue for every divergence detected, appending the finding to the audit store.
- **FR-020**: System MUST publish lead-time, change-failure-rate, and MTTR metrics derived from audit-store events on a rolling 30-day basis.
- **FR-021**: System MUST retain at least the three previous known-good artefact digests per service to guarantee that automated rollback never requires a fresh build.
- **FR-022**: System MUST surface, on every pull request, any open `infrastructure-drift` or evidence-pack reconciliation issue that touches files modified by the pull request.
- **FR-023**: System MUST emit a clear, actionable failure report (with links to the failing job, the offending input, and remediation guidance) for every failed PR gate, deployment gate, rollback, or evidence-pack failure.
- **FR-024**: System MUST refuse to advance any canary stage when the telemetry feeding the SLO burn-rate calculation is unavailable, holding traffic at the current share and raising a tracked issue.
- **FR-025**: System MUST recognise exactly three deployment environments — `dev`, `staging`, and `production` — each with a declared, version-controlled *posture matrix* at the canonical path `infra/policy/posture-matrix.yaml`, describing permitted network exposure, identity/auth modes, and secret-handling rules per Azure resource type. Changes to the posture matrix occur only via pull request to this file; ad-hoc Azure Policy edits made out-of-band in the portal are prohibited and surfaced as drift findings.
- **FR-026**: In `dev`, the posture matrix MAY permit (a) public network access to data-plane services reachable from the public internet (no IP allow-list required; `0.0.0.0/0`), (b) shared-key / API-key / connection-string authentication alongside Managed Identity, and (c) developer-specific Key Vault entries holding short-lived API keys for local development against remote Azure backends. All `dev` resources MUST be deployed into the `rg-ama-dev-{region}` resource group(s), tagged `environment=dev`, and named with a `*-dev-*` token to make accidental cross-environment references trivially detectable. Anonymous access MUST be disabled on every data-plane endpoint (authentication is mandatory even in `dev`), and every `dev` data-plane endpoint MUST emit access logs to the platform observability store. Anomaly detection on dev data-plane access MUST be provided by Microsoft Defender for Cloud (data-plane threat protection enabled on Cosmos DB, Storage, Key Vault, AKS, and Foundry where supported) together with Microsoft Entra ID Protection (risky sign-in / risky user signals on Entra identities used against dev resources). Both services MUST be enabled at the scope that covers the dev environment — i.e. the dev subscription under Topology A, or the single shared subscription under Topology B (in which case the same enablement also covers `staging` and `production`, which is acceptable). Their alerts MUST be routed to the on-call security channel and persisted to the central audit store. No bespoke per-principal threshold logic is required in this feature; dev-specific suppression or tuning is performed in the Defender / Entra ID Protection consoles by the security CODEOWNER.
- **FR-027**: In `staging` and `production`, the posture matrix MUST require (a) private endpoints with public network access disabled on every data-plane service that supports it (Cosmos DB, Storage, Key Vault, Service Bus, Event Grid, Container Registry, Application Insights / Log Analytics ingestion, etc.), (b) Managed Identity / Entra ID workload identity as the only permitted authentication mode, with shared-key / local-auth disabled where the service supports disabling it, and (c) zero literal secrets, connection strings, or API keys in templates or parameter files — only Key Vault references.
- **FR-028**: System MUST, in the IaC policy gate, evaluate every `staging` and `production` change against the posture matrix and fail the PR closed when any posture rule is violated, reporting the specific resource, property, and remediation. The posture-matrix gate is non-skippable in the emergency-hotfix lane.
- **FR-029**: System MUST deny any IaC change that creates a cross-environment reference (e.g., a `staging` template referencing a `dev` resource ID, or a role assignment granting a `dev`-scoped identity access to `production`). Detection is two-layered: (1) at PR time, a static check on the rendered Bicep what-if output uses regex/structural matching against the canonical environment naming tokens (`-dev-`, `-staging-`, `-prod-`) and the target environment's resource group name; (2) at deploy time, a pre-flight Azure Resource Graph query asserts that every resource ID referenced by the deployment lives in the target environment's resource group, failing closed on any traversal. Both layers MUST pass before a deploy proceeds.
- **FR-030**: System MUST rotate `dev` API keys on a defined schedule (default every 24 hours) and revoke prior keys at rotation. `dev` API keys MUST be issued exclusively via a dedicated `gh workflow dispatch` workflow (`dev-key-issue.yml`) that runs under the pipeline's federated OIDC workload identity, mints/rotates the scoped data-plane key via the Azure SDK, writes it to the developer-specific Key Vault path `kv-ama-dev/secrets/devkey-{alias}-{service}` with a 24-hour expiry, revokes the prior key for that `{alias, service}` pair, and emits an append-only audit entry (`actor`, `timestamp`, `action=devkey.issue|rotate`, `service`, `expiry`, `outcome`). No human or service principal MAY hold standing rights to mint `dev` keys outside this workflow. `dev` API keys MUST be scoped to `dev` resources only, MUST be retrievable only from a developer-specific Key Vault path accessible to the developer's own Entra ID identity, and MUST never be valid against `staging` or `production` resources.
- **FR-031**: System MUST, after every deployment, run a post-deploy posture verification that performs a full sweep of every posture-covered resource in the target environment's resource group via an Azure Resource Graph query joined to the posture matrix (`infra/policy/posture-matrix.yaml`), asserting that the actual runtime configuration (network exposure, identity, secret presence) matches the declared posture for that environment. Verification failure on `staging` or `production` MUST fail the deployment and trigger rollback per FR-008; verification failure on `dev` MUST raise a tracked issue but does not block the deploy. The verifier output MUST be included in the evidence pack for every production promotion.
- **FR-032**: System MUST, when a new Azure resource type is introduced, block any deployment of that resource type until an explicit posture rule is added to the matrix and approved by the compliance persona; no resource type may be deployed under an implicit default.
- **FR-033**: System MUST record every posture-matrix exception (e.g., legacy services that cannot disable shared-key auth) as a named entry in `infra/policy/posture-exceptions.yaml` carrying: `name`, `justification`, `compensating_controls`, `owning_team`, `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `dual_approvers` (security + compliance CODEOWNER GitHub handles), and an audit-store pointer. Exceptions MUST be added via pull request requiring dual approval from BOTH the security CODEOWNER and the compliance CODEOWNER. Each exception MUST carry an `expires_at` value no more than 90 days from `issued_at`. A scheduled workflow MUST auto-remove expired entries (after which the underlying posture rule is enforced by the gate); renewal MUST be performed by a fresh dual-approver PR — silent extension is prohibited. Exceptions MUST be enumerable from the audit store, and a quarterly review MUST verify that every active entry has valid dual approval and an unexpired date.

### Key Entities

- **Artefact**: An immutable, signed deployable produced exactly once per merge. Attributes: digest, build run ID, SBOM reference, signature identity, provenance attestation, originating commit, source spec / FR identifiers, `last_known_good_at` timestamp (set when the artefact completes its 100 %-stage observation window without breach and remains unrolled-back).
- **Canary Stage**: A traffic share allocated to a candidate artefact during progressive rollout. Attributes: stage (5 / 25 / 50 / 100 %), entry timestamp, burn-rate window status, decision (advance / hold / rollback).
- **SLO Burn-Rate Window**: A rolling assessment of error-budget consumption against defined SLOs over a one-hour window. Attributes: SLO name, target, measured value, budget consumed, fast-burn / slow-burn status.
- **Evidence Pack**: An immutable bundle assembled per production promotion. Attributes: pack digest, storage URI, retention class, contents inventory, originating artefact digest, deploying actor, approver identities.
- **Audit Event**: An append-only record describing a deployment-related action. Attributes: actor, timestamp, action, rationale, outcome, evidence URI, digest, correlation IDs to incidents and operator events.
- **Change Freeze**: A platform-wide toggle limiting which deployments may proceed. Attributes: state (active / inactive), activator, activation timestamp, reason, deactivation timestamp.
- **Emergency Hotfix Approval**: A two-of-N authorisation captured for a change deployed under freeze. Attributes: change identifier, approvers, timestamps, evidence-pack reconciliation status.
- **Drift Finding**: A delta between live Azure state and Bicep desired state. Attributes: resource ID, property, expected value, observed value, detection timestamp, status (open / resolved).
- **CODEOWNERS Path**: A repository path or pattern bound to required-reviewer personas. Attributes: path glob, required reviewer set, classification (safety-critical, infrastructure, etc.).
- **Pipeline Identity**: A workload identity used by the pipeline to act on cloud resources. Attributes: identity name, federated subject, scope, permissions, last-used timestamp, required Entra ID group memberships (e.g., `ama-oncall-sre`, `ama-incident-commander`, `ama-emergency-approver`) for privileged actions such as freeze invocation and emergency-hotfix approval.
- **Environment Profile**: A named deployment target (`dev`, `staging`, or `production`) with a declared posture. Attributes: name, AU region(s), resource group name (`rg-ama-{env}-{region}`), required tag set (`environment={env}`), naming token (`*-{env}-*`), permitted network exposure modes, permitted authentication modes, secret-handling rules, Azure Policy assignments scoped at the RG.
- **Posture Matrix**: A version-controlled mapping from (Environment Profile × Azure resource type) to a set of policy rules covering network exposure, identity, and secret handling. Single canonical artefact at `infra/policy/posture-matrix.yaml`. Attributes: environment, resource type, network rule, identity rule, secret rule, exception list, last-reviewed date, reviewing personas.
- **Posture Exception**: A named, time-bounded deviation from the posture matrix for a specific resource. Attributes: resource ID, environment, deviating rule, justification, compensating controls, approver identities, review interval, expiry date.
- **Developer API Key**: A short-lived, dev-scoped credential issued for local development against `dev` Azure backends. Attributes: key identifier, owning developer Entra ID identity, issued-at, expires-at (default 24 hours), scope (dev resources only), Key Vault path (`kv-ama-dev/secrets/devkey-{alias}-{service}`), issuing workflow run ID, last-used timestamp, revoked flag.

- **SC-004**: Mean time to restore service after a deployment-induced regression is under 30 minutes during normal operations and under 10 minutes during active incidents.

- **SC-006**: 100 % of cloud resources provisioned by the pipeline reside in an Australian region, except for Microsoft Foundry model-hosting endpoints listed on the named carve-out; any other non-Australian resource attempt is blocked at the gate. The carve-out list is auditable and zero non-Foundry resources outside AU are tolerated.
- **SC-007**: Zero long-lived cloud credentials exist in repository configuration or runners, validated by quarterly audit.
- **SC-008**: 100 % of safety-critical merges (alerting, evacuation, recommendation, content-safety, infrastructure) carry recorded approval from the security and compliance personas.
- **SC-009**: Site reliability engineering toil related to the deployment pipeline (manual deploys, manual rollbacks, manual evidence collection, manual freeze coordination) is below 50 % of SRE capacity. *(Operational metric — measured post-deploy via the SRE observability dashboards delivered under US9 / T072; no direct implementation task.)*

- **SC-017**: 100 % of `dev` API keys have a maximum lifetime of 24 hours and are stored only in developer-specific Key Vault entries; zero `dev` API keys are valid against `staging` or `production` resources.
- **SC-018**: 100 % of posture-matrix exceptions are named, justified, controlled, and reviewed at least quarterly by the compliance persona; zero un-named or un-reviewed exceptions exist in any environment.

## Assumptions

- The platform already exposes service-level telemetry sufficient to compute the SLOs named in FR-007 (availability, p95 API latency, p95 dashboard load, signal freshness, recommendation correctness). If any of these signals are not yet instrumented, instrumentation is treated as a prerequisite outside the scope of this feature.
- The constitution's existing Azure-only, AU-region, OIDC-first, and append-only-audit posture continues to apply. This feature operationalises those obligations rather than introducing them. The single, scoped exception is Microsoft Foundry model-hosting endpoints, where current model availability and quota restrictions in Australian regions justify a carve-out (see FR-018); regulated platform data still resides exclusively in AU regions.
- Container Apps revision-traffic-splitting and App Service deployment-slot swaps are available and configured for each canary-eligible service.
- The platform's audit store accepts append-only writes from the pipeline using the same `actor / timestamp / action / rationale / outcome` schema used for incident and operator events. Where additional deployment-specific fields are required, they are added as optional metadata without breaking the shared schema.
- Two distinct authorised humans are reachable within the organisation's emergency-response window for the emergency-hotfix lane. A single-approver fallback is explicitly out of scope and is treated as an unrecoverable freeze condition.
- "Safety-critical paths" for CODEOWNERS purposes are defined by the existing repository convention: alerting, evacuation, recommendation, content-safety, and infrastructure code paths.
- Image and bundle retention policies are configurable; the minimum retention is three previous known-good digests per service, and the authoritative retention for evidence packs and audit-store entries is 7 years with legal hold (see FR-014). These defaults satisfy Privacy Act §15B, PSPF, and state Emergency Management Act expectations.
- The existing CI workflow (`ci.yml`), CD workflow (`cd.yml`), and drift-detection workflow (`drift-detection.yml`) are the baseline; this feature evolves them in place rather than introducing a parallel pipeline.
- Telemetry, audit-store, and evidence-store regions are constrained to `australiaeast` and `australiasoutheast` per Constitution Principle VI. Microsoft Foundry model-hosting endpoints are exempt from this region constraint (see FR-018); all other data-persisting services remain AU-only.
- The `dev` environment contains no real personal information, no PSPF-marked content, no production telemetry, and no production audit data. All `dev` data is synthetic, masked, or generated; this is what makes the relaxed `dev` posture (public endpoints, API keys) compatible with Constitution NON-NEGOTIABLE #3 (Privacy by design). Any leakage of regulated data into `dev` is treated as a high-severity incident.
- Developers needing access to `staging`-grade data follow a documented just-in-time access pattern (e.g., temporary RBAC via Managed Identity through Azure Bastion or point-to-site VPN); shared keys / public endpoints are NEVER opened on `staging` or `production` for developer convenience.

Assignments are scoped at the **subscription** level under Topology A or at the **resource-group** level under Topology B (per the clarification on environment isolation).

- **Developer access pattern for staging/production**: Azure Bastion plus a hardened jump-host VM per non-prod and prod VNet, with role activation granted just-in-time through Microsoft Entra Privileged Identity Management (PIM) and Managed-Identity-only authentication from the jump-host to backends. No point-to-site VPN, no shared keys, no public endpoints on `staging`/`production`.

## Constraints

- **Constitutional**: All workloads, telemetry, audit, and evidence storage must remain in Australian Azure regions (`australiaeast`, `australiasoutheast`). Microsoft Foundry model-hosting endpoints are explicitly exempt due to current model availability and quota restrictions in AU regions; the exemption is limited to model hosting and does not extend to any other resource type or to regulated data persistence.
- **Identity**: No long-lived cloud credentials anywhere; OIDC-only identity is mandatory.
- **Audit immutability**: Audit-store entries and evidence packs are append-only and immutable for their retention window.
- **Continuity with existing pipeline**: The feature evolves the existing workflows rather than replacing them; existing successful runs must continue to deploy until the new flow is fully cut over.
- **Single source of truth for promotion**: Once an artefact's digest leaves the build job, it must not be rebuilt for downstream environments.
- **No external notification dependency**: As with the predecessor feature, deployment notifications are confined to GitHub-native mechanisms (status checks, deployments, issues) plus the platform audit store; new external notification systems are out of scope.
- **Life-safety primacy**: During an active SEV-1 incident, the change freeze takes precedence over lead-time targets; SC-001 is suspended for the duration of the freeze and is not measured against frozen periods.
- **Environment posture asymmetry**: `dev` posture relaxations (public endpoints, API keys) apply only to `dev`. They are mechanically prevented from leaking into `staging`/`production` by the IaC policy gate (FR-028) and Azure Policy at runtime; no exception process can promote a `dev`-grade configuration into a regulated environment.
- **No regulated data in `dev`**: The `dev` posture relaxation is conditional on the absence of real personal information and PSPF-marked content from `dev`. Any introduction of regulated data into `dev` invalidates the relaxation and triggers a posture re-evaluation.
- **Single shared `dev` topology**: Exactly one shared `dev` environment exists; ephemeral per-PR review environments and per-developer persistent Azure environments are explicitly out of scope. Integration validation against a production-shaped backend continues to rely on `staging`.
