# Cross-border bushfire coordination and NEMA acknowledgement

Bushfires in Australia rarely respect jurisdictional boundaries. The
Black Summer (2019–2020), the 2003 Canberra fires, and the 2009 Black
Saturday fires all involved cross-border evacuation, asset transfer,
and public-information coordination — usually under time pressure and
imperfect intelligence. This article is the operator playbook for the
Hub's **cross-border bushfire** workflow: how to recognise that
cross-border coordination is required, how to invoke the NEMA
acknowledgement step, how the Hub composes per-state sector responses
into a single operating picture, and how to handle the most common
failure modes.

This is **not** a doctrine document — it implements doctrine published
by NEMA, AFAC, and AIDR. Where this article and the source doctrine
disagree, the source doctrine wins; raise an inconsistency via the
article's feedback bar.

## 1. The cross-border indicator

The Hub flags a fire as **cross-border** when **any** of the following
hold:

- The active fire envelope (the convex hull of confirmed perimeter
  observations within the last 60 minutes) intersects more than one
  AU subdivision.
- The 6-hour Bureau of Meteorology fire-weather projection extends
  the fire envelope into a second subdivision with confidence ≥ 0.55.
- An evacuation envelope (declared by any sector front-line persona)
  spans a route that crosses a subdivision boundary.
- A cross-border resource request is open (e.g., NSW RFS requesting
  VIC CFA strike teams).

Any of these conditions sets the fire's `cross_border` flag to
`true`. The flag is visible:

- On the Disaster Map as an `XB` ribbon over the fire pin.
- In the Workspaces tile grid as a *"Cross-border"* badge on the
  affected fire's tile.
- In the Help Assistant's citations when answering questions about
  the fire.

## 2. The NEMA acknowledgement step

When an operator attempts a state-changing action whose effect would
cross a subdivision boundary, the Hub interrupts with the **NEMA
acknowledgement** dialog. The dialog is not a permission gate — the
operator's persona envelope already permits the action — it is a
**doctrinal handshake** that records the Commonwealth's
acknowledgement of the cross-border effect and offers a hand-off
option.

The dialog presents three choices:

1. **Notify NEMA and proceed** — the action commits, with the
   Commonwealth notified asynchronously. The audit row records the
   notification timestamp and the Commonwealth acknowledger (set to
   the on-call NEMA controller; if no on-call is signed in, set to
   the on-call inbox).
2. **Hand off to NEMA** — the action is queued for a Commonwealth
   controller. Your workspace shows the queued state; the
   Commonwealth controller's workspace shows an action banner. The
   action commits with the Commonwealth controller as the actor; you
   are listed as the originator. This is the doctrinally cleanest
   path for genuinely federal-character actions.
3. **Cancel** — no action is taken; the dialog is dismissed; an
   audit row records the cancellation with the *"cross-border
   ack required"* reason.

Every choice is audit-logged. There is no path past the dialog that
does not record an audit row.

## 3. Sector composition

Bushfire response is a **sector** activity — Fire is the lead sector
— but cross-border coordination almost always pulls in Police
(traffic and route security), Medical (evacuation triage), and SES
(wildfire-adjacent flood-risk monitoring). The Hub composes
sector-front-line workspaces into a unified cross-sector view via
the **Cross-Sector** persona.

The Cross-Sector workspace surfaces:

- One row per affected subdivision, with each sector's status as a
  cell.
- A column of co-ordinated actions (e.g., "Issue joint warning to
  routes A1 and B47") that compose into per-sector sub-actions on
  approval.
- A timeline of declarations from each sector's front-line persona.

The Cross-Sector persona may **read** every sector's evidence panel
but may **act** only via the cross-sector actions; per-sector
internal actions (e.g., RFS strike team rotation) remain reserved
for the sector's own operating personas.

## 4. Resource transfers

Cross-border resource transfers (RFS strike teams crossing into VIC,
CFA appliances crossing into NSW, ACT MFB into NSW, etc.) are
**Logistics Officer** actions but require **Operations Officer
co-sign on both sides**. The Hub orchestrates the co-sign chain:

1. Originating Logistics Officer drafts the transfer in the
   **Resource board** workspace.
2. Originating Operations Officer co-signs the export.
3. Receiving Operations Officer co-signs the import.
4. Receiving Logistics Officer accepts and books the resource into
   the receiving sector's resource pool.
5. NEMA acknowledgement is recorded automatically — the transfer is
   inherently cross-border, so the dialog appears at step 1 with the
   *Hand off* option greyed out (a Logistics action is never handed
   to NEMA; only the *Notify NEMA and proceed* path is available).

If the receiving side declines, the resource returns to the
originating pool with no further action; both sides are audit-logged.

## 5. Public information across borders

Public-channel actions across borders are the highest-stakes path.
They invoke:

- The originating PIO drafts the alert in the **Alert Composer**.
- The originating Incident Commander co-signs (within-state amber
  doctrine).
- The Hub detects the cross-border effect (see §1) and presents the
  NEMA acknowledgement dialog.
- The receiving state's Public Information Officer is notified in
  their workspace banner; they may either *Mirror* the alert into
  their state's channels or *Issue a paired alert* with their own
  framing (typically used when the linguistic register differs, e.g.,
  alpine vs coastal terminology).
- Both alerts publish simultaneously when the receiving PIO co-signs.
- A single correlation ID binds the originating draft, both
  approvals, both publications, the NEMA acknowledgement, and any
  subsequent revision.

If the receiving PIO does **not** co-sign within the configured
window (default 5 minutes; tunable per tenant by the constitutional
steward), the originating alert publishes alone with a *"Receiving
state did not co-publish"* annotation in the audit trail. The
publication still occurs because public-safety doctrine requires
that the originating state act on its own evidence; the cross-border
co-publication is a coordination optimisation, not a precondition.

## 6. Evacuation envelopes

Evacuation envelopes that cross subdivision boundaries are declared
in the **Field Coordinator** workspace (offline-first), confirmed by
the Operations Officer, and trigger:

- Notification to the receiving subdivision's Police sector for
  route-security planning.
- Notification to the receiving subdivision's Medical sector for
  triage capacity planning.
- A standing offer of accommodation co-ordination from the receiving
  subdivision's Logistics Officer.

The Hub does not **direct** the receiving subdivision; it
notifies, surfaces, and records. Direction of the receiving
subdivision's response is reserved for the receiving subdivision's
operating personas.

## 7. The 30-minute rule

Bushfire intelligence ages quickly under wind shifts. The Hub
enforces a **30-minute freshness** rule on cross-border actions: any
evidence card older than 30 minutes overlays a *"Stale evidence"*
badge and the action button refuses with *"Refresh evidence to
proceed"*. This is stricter than the general 60-minute rule because
cross-border decisions cascade through more receivers.

To refresh:

- Click the evidence card's refresh control. The card re-pulls the
  underlying signal from Cosmos and stamps the new observation
  time.
- If the underlying signal itself is stale (no new observations
  upstream), the refresh changes nothing and the badge persists.
  This is doctrinally correct — you cannot manufacture freshness.

## 8. Failure modes

### 8.1 NEMA on-call inbox is unreachable

If the on-call NEMA controller is not signed in **and** the on-call
inbox configuration is missing, the *Notify NEMA and proceed* path
queues the notification with an *"undelivered"* annotation. The
action still commits — public-safety doctrine wins — but the
notification appears as a constitutional alarm on the Governance
Dashboard until delivered.

### 8.2 Receiving state's Hub is offline

A receiving state's Hub being unreachable does not block the
originating state. The mirror/co-publish handshake degrades to a
*"Receiving state offline"* annotation; the originating publication
proceeds. When the receiving state recovers, the cross-border
banner asks the receiving PIO whether to retroactively mirror.

### 8.3 Conflicting envelopes from two states

If two states independently declare evacuation envelopes that
overlap with conflicting directions of travel, the Cross-Sector
workspace surfaces both with a *"Conflicting evacuation directions"*
banner. The Hub does **not** auto-resolve. The two states' Operations
Officers (and, if escalated, NEMA) negotiate via the cross-sector
chat; the resolution is recorded as a single audit row co-signed by
both states.

### 8.4 Time-zone confusion

Subdivisions cross time-zone boundaries (the Eyre Highway is
notorious). The Hub displays every cross-border action's timestamps
in **both** the originating and receiving subdivision's display
time, with UTC available on hover. Operators should never schedule
relative to wall-clock time across borders without confirming the
zone.

## 9. Worked example

A NSW RFS controller observes a Snowy Mountains fire's projection
extending into VIC's alpine area within 4 hours.

1. The controller refreshes the fire-weather panel; the projection
   is amber (0.71). The Hub flags the fire as `cross_border`.
2. The controller drafts an evacuation envelope spanning Khancoban
   (NSW) and Corryong (VIC). Field Coordinator confirms.
3. NSW Operations Officer initiates a public alert in the Alert
   Composer, citing the projection. Amber co-sign with NSW Incident
   Commander completes.
4. The cross-border NEMA acknowledgement dialog appears. The IC
   chooses *Hand off to NEMA* because the projection's
   confidence is amber and the alpine area is a federal park.
5. The on-call NEMA controller receives the action banner;
   acknowledges; co-publishes with the VIC PIO via the
   cross-sector mirror handshake.
6. Both alerts publish simultaneously. The audit trail shows a
   single correlation ID with: NSW draft, NSW IC co-sign, NEMA
   acknowledgement, VIC PIO co-publish, both publications.
7. Resource transfer (NSW RFS → VIC alpine) completes via the
   Logistics Officer co-sign chain over the next 90 minutes.
