# Observer mode — a complete walkthrough

Observer mode is the read-only, full-visibility posture used by
auditors, post-incident reviewers, AAO scrutiny teams, executive
oversight, and content authors who need to verify that a doctrinal
change is reflected end-to-end. It is not a "spectator" view — it is
the **scrutiny instrument**. This article is the operational guide:
how to enter, what you see, what you can and cannot do, how to
record findings, and how to leave a clean audit trail of your own
review.

If you are reading this because you have been assigned to a
post-incident review, start at §3 (Reviewing an incident) after
reading §1 and §2 to ground.

## 1. Entering Observer mode

Observer mode is a **persona**, not a switch. You enter it by
signing in with the `observer-auditor` claim. If your IdP grants
both an operating persona and `observer-auditor`, the persona
switcher (`Alt+P`) lets you move between them at will; switching
into Observer is audit-logged, switching out is audit-logged.

You **cannot** combine Observer with an operating persona in a
single envelope. The Constitution requires the separation; an
Observer who could also act would defeat the scrutiny model.

On entry to Observer:

- The default landing surface becomes `/governance/audit` (not the
  Disaster Map).
- An *"Observer"* watermark appears on every page; it is also
  audible on screen-reader page-load announcements.
- Every state-changing control is hidden. The screens still render
  fully — you see what an operating persona would see — but
  buttons that would commit a change are absent.
- You gain read access to **red-band** signals that operating
  personas cannot see.
- The *Evidence pack* exporter appears in the page header.

If your IdP claim grants `observer-auditor` and you do not see the
watermark, the persona is misconfigured. Sign out and back in; if
the watermark is still missing, raise an incident immediately —
this is a constitutional gate breach.

## 2. The Observer landing — `/governance/audit`

The audit timeline is the workhorse of Observer mode. The
[`audit-observer-mode`](/help/articles/audit-observer-mode) article
documents the timeline UI in full; here we cover the
Observer-specific affordances.

### 2.1 The Observer header bar

Above the timeline, Observer mode shows:

- **Active scrutiny** — a one-line note about why you are in
  Observer mode (e.g., *"AAO scrutiny: 2026-Q1 readiness"*). The
  note is set by the scrutiny lead at session start; it appears in
  the audit-row metadata of any evidence-pack export so reviewers
  downstream understand the context.
- **Scrutiny scope** — the persona / jurisdiction / time-window
  filters declared at scrutiny start. The timeline filter rail
  defaults to this scope; you can widen but doing so prompts a
  *"Scope expansion?"* dialog and audit-logs the expansion.
- **Findings inbox** — a small badge counting findings you have
  recorded so far in this session.

### 2.2 The findings recorder

Findings are first-class artefacts. As you scrutinise the
timeline, you can record findings without leaving the timeline:

- Press `n` (focus on a row) to attach a *Finding* to that row.
- Choose a **finding kind**: `observation`, `concern`, `recommendation`,
  `compliance gap`, `commendation`.
- Add a free-text rationale. Rationale is **not** PII-redacted; it
  goes into the evidence pack verbatim, so write it knowing it
  will be read by external reviewers.
- The finding records: timestamp, your identity, the row's
  correlation ID, the kind, the rationale, and the active scrutiny
  note.

Findings appear in the *Findings inbox* and on the right rail of
the source row. They are visible to other Observer-mode users in
the same scrutiny scope; they are **not** visible to operating
personas.

## 3. Reviewing an incident

A typical post-incident review follows this arc:

1. **Define scope.** The scrutiny lead sets the persona /
   jurisdiction / time window in `/governance/scrutiny/new`. The
   scope is recorded as a *scrutiny ticket*; every audit row
   accessed during the review is bound to the ticket.
2. **Walk the timeline forward.** Start at the scope's earliest
   row. Read each row's detail panel, including pre-state and
   post-state snapshots. Record findings as you go.
3. **Walk the correlation graph.** For high-significance rows,
   click *Open correlation graph* to see the DAG of related rows.
   The graph helps reconstruct multi-step workflows (signal →
   recommendation → approval → publication → community response).
4. **Cross-reference the doctrinal snapshot.** Each row records
   the help-snapshot version in force at action time. Open the
   snapshot via the row's *Snapshot* link; verify that the
   action's interpretation matches the doctrine in force *at that
   time*, not the current doctrine.
5. **Verify the hash chain.** Each row's hash-chain dot is green
   if the row's `prev_hash` matches the recomputed hash; red on
   mismatch. A red dot in your scrutiny window is a constitutional
   incident in itself; record it as a `concern` finding and
   escalate to the constitutional steward immediately.
6. **Compose the evidence pack.** When the walk is complete,
   *Generate evidence pack* from the scrutiny ticket. The pack
   includes every row in scope, every finding you recorded, the
   relevant doctrinal snapshot, and a signed manifest. The pack is
   in your *Evidence-pack inbox* (`/governance/evidence/inbox`).

The Hub does **not** generate a narrative report from the
findings — that remains a human task. The pack is the raw material
for the report; the report itself is authored outside the Hub.

## 4. What you can read

Observer mode reads **everything** within the scrutiny scope:

- Audit rows, including refusal rows and DLQ rows.
- Pre-state and post-state snapshots referenced by audit rows.
- All confidence bands (green, amber, **red**), at the value they
  carried at action time.
- Calibration history — the calibration plot's evolution over the
  scope's time window.
- Snapshot publication and rollback events.
- Configuration changes (threshold tuning, support-handoff
  inbox edits, persona claim changes).
- Help-corpus content as it was at any point in the scope.

## 5. What you cannot do

Observer cannot:

- Take any state-changing action — no approvals, no publications,
  no resource transfers, no threshold tuning, no claim changes.
- Author or edit help articles (content authoring is a separate
  persona).
- Issue chat prompts that would invoke the gateway's deny path
  (sensitive operational, PSPF, PII) — Observer's gateway profile
  is the strictest available.
- Read content **outside** the scrutiny scope without expanding
  the scope (which prompts a confirmation dialog and audit-logs
  the expansion).
- Forge or replay audit rows. The Hub has no Observer-only path
  past the append-only model.

## 6. Reading findings other Observers recorded

The findings inbox shows **your** findings by default. Switch to
*All findings (this scope)* to see every finding any Observer in
the scrutiny ticket has recorded. Findings are not editable by
other Observers — each Observer owns their findings. You may add
*"agreement"* or *"divergence"* annotations to another's finding;
those annotations are themselves audit-logged and threaded under
the original finding.

## 7. Recording cross-cutting findings

Some findings span multiple rows (e.g., *"Repeated cross-border
publications without NEMA acknowledgement, rows X, Y, Z"*).
Record these from the *Findings* inbox by clicking *New
cross-cutting finding* and listing the implicated correlation
IDs. The Hub validates that every listed correlation ID is in the
scrutiny scope; out-of-scope IDs require a scope expansion (see
§2.1).

## 8. Leaving Observer mode

Switching out of Observer (or signing out) closes the scrutiny
session. The Hub:

- Marks the scrutiny ticket as *closed* with your sign-out
  timestamp.
- Aggregates your findings into the ticket's findings record.
- If you have an unexported evidence pack, prompts you to either
  generate it now or save the scrutiny ticket as *partial*.
- Audit-logs the session close.

You can re-enter the same scrutiny ticket later (the ticket is
yours until handed off) and the timeline filters and findings
restore.

## 9. Hand-offs between Observers

Long-running scrutinies (multi-day AAO reviews, multi-week
post-incident analyses) are typically shared across Observer
shifts. Hand-offs use the *Hand off* button on the scrutiny
ticket:

1. Choose the receiving Observer from a dropdown of currently
   signed-in `observer-auditor` personas.
2. Add a hand-off note (free text; audit-logged) summarising
   findings to date, open lines of inquiry, and suggested next
   steps.
3. The receiving Observer's banner shows the hand-off; on
   acceptance, the ticket is theirs and your view becomes
   read-only on the ticket (you keep your individual findings).

Hand-offs are reversible by mutual consent: either party can
*Return ticket*, restoring the prior owner.

## 10. Interaction with the Help Assistant

In Observer mode, the Help Assistant is grounded against:

- The same help corpus as operating personas.
- The audit-row content within your scrutiny scope (which
  operating personas do not have access to).

This means an Observer can ask, e.g., *"Why was warning W-2026-0193
revised after publication?"*, and the assistant can quote the
revision audit row inline. It cannot, however, quote rows outside
your scrutiny scope; widening the scope is required first.

The Observer's chat prompts and refusals are recorded with the
*"observer-auditor / scrutiny-ticket-id"* metadata so external
reviewers can later see what lines of inquiry the Observer pursued.
