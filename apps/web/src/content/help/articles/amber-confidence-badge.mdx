# Confidence bands and the doctrine of red suppression

Every signal, recommendation, and projection surfaced in the AMA Hub is
stamped with a **confidence band** — a single trichotomy (`green`,
`amber`, `red`) derived from the underlying model's calibrated
probability. The band drives both the visual treatment in the UI and a
constitutional rule about which personas may *act* on it. This article
documents the bands, the calibration, the suppression doctrine, the
configuration knobs, and the audit consequences of every interaction
with an amber or red artefact.

If you only have thirty seconds, the rules are:

- **Green ≥ 0.85** — act normally.
- **Amber 0.55 – 0.85** — act with a second-pair-of-eyes for
  state-changing operations.
- **Red < 0.55** — hard-suppressed in every workspace except **Risk
  Manager**. Red signals never reach an Operations or Incident
  Commander workspace.

## 1. Where the numbers come from

Confidence is a **calibrated** probability, not a raw model output. The
calibration pipeline (spec-036, retrieval & gateway components) takes
the model's logit, applies isotonic regression against a tenant-specific
ground-truth set, and produces a probability that — within ±5 % across
the operating range — equals the empirical hit rate.

Calibration is re-fitted **monthly** from the prior 90 days of
ground-truth labels. The fit timestamp is on every confidence-bearing
artefact's evidence card; if it is older than 60 days, the artefact
overlays a *"calibration stale"* badge and is treated as one band lower
than its raw value.

The Risk Manager workspace surfaces the calibration plot for the
current tenant. If the plot's reliability diagram drifts substantially
from the diagonal, raise an incident — the model is mis-calibrated and
the bands are no longer doctrinally meaningful.

## 2. Why the thresholds are where they are

The 0.85 / 0.55 thresholds are not arbitrary. They were chosen against
two reference operating points:

- **0.85** is the operating point at which the marginal cost of a
  false positive (acting on a non-event) equals the marginal cost of a
  false negative (failing to act on a real event), under the
  Commonwealth's tabletop assumptions for moderate hazards.
- **0.55** is the operating point below which the model performs
  statistically no better than the previous-decade base rate. Acting on
  a sub-0.55 signal is acting on noise.

The thresholds **may be tuned** by the Risk Manager for a tenant, but
only within ±0.05 of the defaults and only with constitutional steward
acknowledgement. See *§5 Tuning* below.

## 3. Visual treatment

### 3.1 In the map

- **Green** pin: full-saturation icon in the layer's hue.
- **Amber** pin: same icon with a thin amber halo and a "★" overstrike
  that screen readers announce as "amber band".
- **Red** pin: rendered only in Risk Manager view. In all other
  workspaces, red signals are filtered server-side and never reach the
  client, so a malicious console hack cannot reveal them.

### 3.2 In the recommendation cards

- **Green** card: action button enabled.
- **Amber** card: action button enabled but flanked by a *"Requires
  second eyes"* annotation; on click, a co-signer dialog appears.
- **Red** card: not present in operating workspaces. In Risk Manager,
  rendered with a red border and a *"Risk-only — not actionable"*
  ribbon; the action button is replaced with *"Promote to amber"*
  (which triggers a band-promotion review).

### 3.3 In the audit trail

Every state-changing action records the confidence band of the
artefact at the moment of action, **plus** the calibration timestamp.
This is the only way to reconstruct, after the fact, what the operator
actually saw.

### 3.4 In the chat assistant

When the assistant cites a confidence-bearing artefact, it includes the
band as part of the citation (e.g., *"per signal `sig-9af3` (amber,
0.71)"*). If the cited band is amber or red, the assistant emits a
chat-side *"Caveat: amber confidence; verify with primary source"*
prefix.

## 4. Doctrine: who can act on what

| Persona              | Green | Amber                | Red               |
| -------------------- | ----- | -------------------- | ----------------- |
| Incident Commander   | ✓     | ✓ (co-sign required) | ✗ (suppressed)    |
| Operations Officer   | ✓     | ✓ (co-sign required) | ✗ (suppressed)    |
| Planning Officer     | ✓     | ✓ (read + plan)      | ✗ (suppressed)    |
| Public Information Officer | ✓ | ✓ (co-sign required, PIO+IC) | ✗ (suppressed) |
| Logistics Officer    | ✓     | ✓ (co-sign required) | ✗ (suppressed)    |
| Field Coordinator    | ✓     | ✓ (capture only; no public action) | ✗ (suppressed) |
| Risk Manager         | ✓     | ✓                    | ✓ (review only; no operational action) |
| Resilience Planner   | ✓     | ✓ (planning only)    | ✗ (suppressed)    |
| Observer/Auditor     | ✓     | ✓                    | ✓ (read-only)     |
| Executive Leader     | ✓     | ✓ (read-only)        | ✓ (read-only)     |

Co-signing means a second persona of the same or higher decision-rights
envelope must approve before the action commits. The co-signer is
selected from a dropdown of currently signed-in eligible personas; if
none are available, the action queues until one signs in. The co-sign
relationship is audit-logged on both sides.

## 5. Tuning

The Risk Manager can tune the bands within ±0.05 of the defaults via
**Workspace → Risk → Threshold tuning**. Tuning steps:

1. Open the threshold tuning panel. The current and proposed thresholds
   are shown side-by-side over the calibration plot.
2. Drag the green/amber separator (default 0.85) within `[0.80, 0.90]`.
3. Drag the amber/red separator (default 0.55) within `[0.50, 0.60]`.
4. Click *Preview impact*. The Hub replays the prior 30 days of signals
   under the proposed thresholds and reports the change in:
   - Volume of amber-band actions.
   - Volume of red-suppressed signals.
   - Estimated false-positive and false-negative rate change.
5. If you choose *Apply*, a constitutional steward acknowledgement
   prompt appears. The tuning takes effect only after acknowledgement
   and is audit-logged with both actor and steward identities.

Tuning is reversible. The tuning history is on the Governance Dashboard.

## 6. Promotion and demotion

Risk Manager can **promote** a red signal to amber (after manual
review) or **demote** a green signal to amber (typically because of a
contradicting observation). Both transitions:

- Record an audit row with the originating Risk Manager, the rationale
  text, and the prior/new band.
- Take effect for all operating workspaces immediately; the WebSocket
  push refreshes affected pins within ~1 s.
- Are reversible by the same Risk Manager within 30 minutes; thereafter
  a constitutional steward acknowledgement is required.

Promotion is intentionally heavyweight to avoid an "amber inflation"
spiral where every red signal eventually becomes amber.

## 7. The "stale calibration" overlay

If the calibration fit timestamp is older than 60 days, every
confidence-bearing artefact in the affected tenant overlays a *"stale
calibration"* badge. The Hub treats stale-calibration artefacts as
**one band lower** than their nominal value:

- Stale **green** ⇒ amber treatment (co-sign required for action).
- Stale **amber** ⇒ red treatment (suppressed in operating workspaces).
- Stale **red** ⇒ unchanged (still suppressed).

Stale calibration is a constitutional gate: a red Calibration gate on
the Governance Dashboard halts state-changing operations until
re-calibration completes.

## 8. Confidence in chat citations

When the chat assistant grounds in a confidence-bearing source, the
citation badge encodes the band:

- Green badge with check: high-confidence citation.
- Amber badge with caret: amber citation; the assistant prepends a
  *"verify with primary source"* caveat.
- Red badge with halt: never seen in chat — the gateway suppresses red
  citations before they reach the assistant.

## 9. Frequently asked

**Why don't I see the raw probability?**
Operationally, raw probabilities encourage spurious precision and
inter-operator inconsistency. The bands map to action doctrine;
probabilities map to nothing useful at the workspace level. Risk
Manager has the raw values.

**Why is amber co-sign sometimes blocked?**
If no eligible co-signer is signed in, the action queues. You can hand
off via *Send to <persona>* or wait. Queued actions are visible in your
workspace inbox.

**Can I bypass amber co-sign in an emergency?**
No. The Constitution is explicit: amber requires two-person concurrence
for state-changing operations. If you cannot find a co-signer, escalate
to Incident Commander or invoke SupportHandoff.

**A red signal looks important — can I see it?**
Only Risk Manager, Observer/Auditor, and Executive Leader can see red
signals. If you genuinely believe a red signal needs operational
action, ask Risk Manager to **promote** it.
