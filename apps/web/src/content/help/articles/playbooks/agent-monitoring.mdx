# Playbook: working with the monitoring agent

This playbook is the operator's companion to the
[Monitoring agent workspace](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/agent-monitoring).
It covers how to triage a busy queue, when to acknowledge vs
escalate vs dismiss, how to read the reasoning trace, and how
the agent should and should not influence your situational
awareness.

The monitoring agent is the **first responder to data** in the
Hub. It is reading every signal feed continuously; you are not.
Your job is to verify the agent's interpretation against your
operational judgement, not to replace it.

## 1. The first sixty seconds on shift

When you take a shift, your first sixty seconds in the
monitoring workspace should be:

1. Glance the *Last ingestion* timestamp. If > 2 minutes stale,
   raise the feed health concern in the workspace's *Feed
   health* drill-in.
2. Glance the recommendation queue depth. If > 20, you are
   inheriting a triage backlog; plan accordingly.
3. Sort the queue by *recency × severity × confidence* (the
   default).
4. Read the top three recommendations end-to-end including the
   reasoning trace. Form a baseline mental model of what the
   agent currently believes.
5. Move to the operating picture and verify with two or three
   independent signal sources (the agent's drivers plus one
   you choose at random).

If the agent's mental model and your forming-up mental model
disagree at this point, *don't act on the agent yet*. Build
your own understanding first.

## 2. Triaging the queue

For each recommendation:

- Read the **subject artefact**. If you don't know the
  geography, look at the operating picture.
- Read the **reasoning trace**. Pay attention to the cited
  signals' confidence bands. Amber drivers in a green
  recommendation deserve a second look.
- Decide:
  - **Acknowledge** if the recommendation is sound but no
    immediate action is needed (the agent is asking you to
    *watch* something).
  - **Escalate** if the recommendation needs another persona's
    envelope to action (e.g., a *prepare-evacuation*
    recommendation needs Field Coordinator attention; an
    *escalate-to-risk* needs Risk Manager).
  - **Dismiss** if the recommendation is unsound *and you can
    articulate why*. Always provide rationale; the rationale
    trains the agent's relevance model and the audit trail
    captures it.

Do **not** sit on recommendations indefinitely. An
unacknowledged amber recommendation aged > 30 minutes is a
small audit-row red flag; > 2 hours is a larger one.

## 3. When the agent disagrees with you

The agent and the operator will disagree often. Doctrine:

- The agent is reading *more data faster* than you can. Trust
  it on volume.
- You are reading *context the agent does not have* (an old-
  timer's anecdote about the prevailing wind on Sunday
  afternoons in this valley; a known anomaly in the BoM
  station at this site). Trust yourself on context.
- When you disagree, **dismiss with rationale**. The rationale
  goes into the agent's training signal; over time the agent
  learns the contextual exceptions.
- If you find yourself dismissing the same kind of
  recommendation repeatedly, raise it with Risk Manager —
  systematic disagreement may indicate a calibration drift.

## 4. When the agent is silent

The absence of a recommendation is itself information. If you
expect the agent to be flagging something (e.g., the BoM
forecast you read this morning predicted Severe fire weather)
and the agent's queue is empty, two possibilities:

- The agent's interpretation is correct and your prior
  expectation was overly cautious.
- The agent has missed something — feed offline, signal
  upstream gap, model failure mode.

Distinguish via the operating picture: open the relevant
hazard layer, look at the underlying signals. If the signals
show the situation but the agent isn't recommending, raise
with Risk Manager.

## 5. Cross-jurisdictional triage

Monitoring agent recommendations are scoped to your active
jurisdiction filter (see
[`screen-by-screen/multi-jurisdiction-filter`](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/multi-jurisdiction-filter)).
For cross-border situational awareness:

- Widen your filter to include adjacent jurisdictions for the
  shift if doctrinally allowed (your IdP claim must include
  them).
- Watch for cross-border markers on recommendations
  (the cross-border ribbon on the subject artefact).
- For genuine cross-border events, expect the
  Incident Commander or Operations Officer to coordinate via
  the cross-sector chat in addition to the Hub.

## 6. Calibration health awareness

Check the calibration timestamp at shift start (Governance
Dashboard's calibration panel, or the agent workspace's header
if exposed). If calibration is > 50 days old (approaching the
60-day floor), expect more amber recommendations and
co-sign-required actions; plan accordingly. If > 60 days,
raise with Risk Manager.

## 7. The agent in degraded states

If the agent is *Paused*: existing recommendations remain;
no new emissions. Operating picture continues to update from
feeds; you are still responsible for situational awareness.
Pause is usually an Operations Officer choice during
controlled testing or when a known model defect is being
patched.

If the agent is *Failed*: existing recommendations remain;
no new emissions; the *Last ingestion* may still be recent
(monitoring continues; only the recommendation engine has
failed). Treat as a partial outage; coordinate with Risk
Manager.

## 8. Audit hygiene

Every action you take in the workspace audit-logs. The audit
trail captures *what* you did but not *why* unless you provide
a rationale. Best practice:
- *Dismiss* always with rationale.
- *Acknowledge* with rationale when the recommendation is
  on something you've decided to monitor under your own
  attention rather than escalating to another persona.
- *Escalate* with rationale when the receiving persona may
  not understand why the escalation arrived (e.g., novel
  cross-sector escalation).

## 9. Handover

At end of shift:
- Acknowledge or dismiss any aged recommendations rather than
  leaving them as inheritance.
- Brief the incoming operator verbally (out-of-Hub) on:
  - The current operating-picture posture.
  - Any agent recommendations you've been actively watching.
  - Any agent-disagreement you've noted.
- Optionally export an evidence pack of the shift's audit rows
  for your own records.
