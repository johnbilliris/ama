# Playbook: working with the resource-allocation agent

This playbook complements the
[Resource-allocation agent workspace](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/agent-resource-allocation).
It covers the dispatch chain from agent recommendation to
booked transfer, the mutual-aid agreements model, cross-border
specifics, and the most common pitfalls.

The resource agent is an **optimiser**, not a director. Its
recommendations balance demand against availability under the
standing mutual-aid agreements. Operators accept, amend, or
reject recommendations in the dispatch chain; the agent never
moves a unit on its own.

## 1. Reading a recommendation

Each recommendation carries:
- A **kind** — `move`, `pre-position`, `release`,
  `request-mutual-aid`, `decline-mutual-aid-request`.
- A **source** — the unit(s) being moved.
- A **destination** — the location, sector, or jurisdiction.
- An **urgency** — *Immediate* / *Within 6 h* / *Within 24 h*.
- A **confidence band**.
- A **mutual-aid path** — intra-sector / cross-sector
  intra-state / cross-border / international.
- **Drivers** — top 3 signals or assessments.

Read in this order: kind → source → destination → mutual-aid
path → urgency → drivers. Open the mutual-aid drawer to
verify the agreement's standing terms.

## 2. The dispatch chain

The chain has five steps; the agent's role is just step 1
(propose). The remaining steps are operator actions:

1. **Agent proposes** — recommendation appears in the queue.
2. **Operations Officer forwards** to Logistics Officer. The
   forwarding records the OO's intent; cross-border
   forwarding triggers NEMA acknowledgement.
3. **Originating Logistics Officer drafts** the transfer in
   the *Resource board* workspace at `/workspaces/logistics`.
4. **Originating Operations Officer co-signs** the export.
   Cross-border requires receiving Operations Officer
   co-signing the import.
5. **Receiving Logistics Officer accepts** and books the
   resource into the receiving pool.

Each step is audit-logged. The transfer's correlation ID
binds the agent's proposal, every co-sign, and the eventual
unit movement (where systems-of-record are integrated).

## 3. Mutual-aid agreements

Mutual-aid agreements are the legal and operational framework
for resource sharing. The Hub's agreements catalogue at
`/governance/agreements` lists every active agreement with:
- The parties and effective dates.
- The standing terms (cost recovery, command-and-control
  transfer rules, recall conditions).
- The activation triggers.
- The contact roles on each side.

The resource agent's recommendations cite the relevant
agreement; the mutual-aid drawer in the workspace surfaces it
with one click. Always verify the agreement is active before
forwarding (rare cases of an agreement having expired without
the catalogue refreshing).

## 4. Cross-border specifics

Cross-border resource transfers always involve NEMA
acknowledgement. The recommendation's mutual-aid path
indicates `cross-border`; on forward, the NEMA dialog
appears.

The doctrinal sequence:
1. Originating Operations Officer forwards. NEMA dialog
   appears with *Notify and proceed* and *Hand off* options.
   For most resource transfers, *Notify and proceed* is the
   right path (resource transfer is not a federal-character
   action; it's a state-to-state coordination).
2. Originating Logistics Officer drafts; originating
   Operations Officer co-signs export.
3. Receiving Operations Officer co-signs import.
4. Receiving Logistics Officer accepts.

The receiving side typically responds within 5 minutes
during active operations.

## 5. Pre-positioning vs reactive movement

The agent recommends both:
- **Pre-positioning** — moving units toward a likely-impact
  area before the impact occurs. Improves response time but
  commits resources speculatively.
- **Reactive movement** — moving units in response to an
  active impact.

Pre-positioning recommendations carry a `pre-position` kind
and an *Within 24 h* or *Within 6 h* urgency. Forward
selectively; over-pre-positioning depletes the originating
sector's reserves for *its* potential events.

## 6. Releasing units

The `release` recommendation tells you a unit can return to
the originating pool because the operational driver has
diminished. Forward releases as readily as movements:
unreleased units sit in receiving pools, depleting the
originating sector unnecessarily.

Release also triggers a return-leg logistics consideration
(fuel, crew rotation, deadhead time). The Logistics Officer
considers the return path during the release co-sign.

## 7. Mutual-aid request received

When a peer sector or jurisdiction requests resources from
your sector, the request appears in your workspace as a
`request-mutual-aid` recommendation (the agent's
recommendation on how to respond) or as an inbound request
in the Resource board.

Either way, the response is:
- Accept and dispatch via the standard chain.
- Decline with rationale (e.g., your own active commitments
  preclude release).
- Counter-offer (different units, different timing).

Audit-logged regardless. The peer's expectation should be
managed verbally if the response will be slow.

## 8. International assistance

For OS jurisdiction (Pacific assistance) the chain is the
same but with two additional steps:
- **MFA diplomatic note** — the Department of Foreign Affairs
  and Trade signs off on the international transfer; the
  agent's recommendation cites the relevant diplomatic note
  number.
- **Customs and biosecurity** — physical movement requires
  customs clearance and biosecurity inspection on both ends.
  The Hub doesn't manage these directly; the Logistics
  Officer co-ordinates with the relevant border agencies.

International recommendations are rare and slow; expect
24-hour-plus turnaround.

## 9. Edge cases

### 9.1 Conflicting recommendations
Two recommendations proposing opposite movements of the
same unit (e.g., move to A, simultaneously move to B). The
queue surfaces the conflict with a paired view; resolve by
choosing one and dismissing the other with rationale.

### 9.2 Unit recall in transit
A unit dispatched but in transit can be recalled by the
originating Operations Officer with a *Recall* action. The
recall is audit-logged and the unit reverts to the
originating pool. Coordinate verbally — radio is faster
than the Hub for in-transit recalls.

### 9.3 Receiving side capacity exceeded
If the receiving Logistics Officer determines the
receiving pool cannot absorb the inbound (e.g., no
secure parking, no crew accommodation), they decline.
The originating side considers an alternative recipient
or holds the unit.

### 9.4 Unit unavailable mid-chain
A unit recommended at step 1 may become unavailable by step
3 (mechanical failure, crew sickness, recall to a higher-
priority event). The Resource board surfaces the
unavailability; the chain restarts with an alternative unit.

### 9.5 Cost recovery dispute
Cost recovery is governed by the standing mutual-aid
agreement. Disputes happen post-event, not during dispatch.
The Hub's audit trail of the dispatch chain is the
authoritative record for cost-recovery accounting.
