# Playbook: designing and running an exercise

This playbook is the operator's guide to designing, running, and
debriefing exercises in the Hub. It assumes you are a controller
or co-controller (Incident Commander or Operations Officer
envelope on the exercise tenant) and that you have read
[`screen-by-screen/simulation-control`](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/simulation-control)
for the surface reference.

Exercises in the Hub are not toys: every action produces real
audit rows in the exercise tenant's container, replay is bit-
deterministic, and evidence packs exported from exercises have
the same external-review value as production packs (with the
*"exercise"* environment badge). Treat exercise design with the
same rigour as production operations.

## 1. Choose the exercise mode

The Hub supports four exercise modes:

- **Tabletop** — discussion-based; no live agency systems
  involved. Participants sit in a shared room or call. The
  Hub provides the operating picture and the agent
  recommendations; participants discuss decisions verbally.
  Audit captures the verbal decisions via the controller's
  *"declared decision"* affordance.
- **Functional** — live agency systems are involved (e.g., the
  Bureau provides real fire-weather observations into the
  exercise tenant) but no resources actually move. Participants
  exercise their dispatch and command paths in the Hub; agency
  systems-of-record receive the dispatches and acknowledge but
  no physical movement occurs.
- **Live-fire** — real resources move. Used for drills,
  training events with physical activation. Coordinate carefully
  with the affected community.
- **Mixed** — any combination, scoped per sector or
  jurisdiction. Most realistic exercises end up here.

Mode is recorded at exercise creation; it determines which
mutual-aid agreements are *active* (live-fire) vs *acknowledged
but not booked* (functional/tabletop).

## 2. Define the scenario

Scenario design has six axes:

1. **Hazard class** — fire / flood / cyclone / heatwave /
   earthquake / multi-hazard.
2. **Geographic envelope** — the affected area; can span
   subdivisions for cross-border practice.
3. **Temporal envelope** — duration; tabletops typically run
   1–4 hours of exercise time, often warp-compressed.
4. **Severity arc** — the trajectory of the scenario over
   exercise time; rising / steady / declining / oscillating.
5. **Cross-sector dependencies** — which sectors are involved
   and at what handoff points.
6. **Learning objectives** — what specifically you want
   participants to practise.

Capture all six in the exercise's metadata at creation. The
audit trail records each axis at action time; reviewers use
them to bound their analysis.

## 3. Seed the scenario

Two seeding paths (see
[`screen-by-screen/simulation-control`](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/simulation-control)):

- **Template seeding** for deterministic reproducibility — the
  same template ID produces bit-identical signals across runs.
  Use for assessed exercises where bias from random variation
  must be eliminated.
- **Natural-language seeding** for novel scenarios — the
  controller chats with the seeding agent (*"Seed a Riverina
  flood with NSW–VIC cross-border evacuation across the Murray,
  peak in 6 hours, amber confidence"*) and the agent produces
  signal fixtures matching the description. Use for training
  exercises where novelty is itself a learning objective.

Either way, the seed is **frozen** once exercise time starts.
Mid-exercise injections (see §6) are separate audit events.

## 4. Set warp ratio

Warp ratios let you compress hours of operational time into a
session. Options: 1× (real-time), 2×, 4×, 8×, 16×, 32×.

Guidance:
- **1×** for live-fire and for high-fidelity tabletop debriefs
  where wall-clock decision latency is itself the learning
  objective.
- **2×–4×** for typical functional exercises.
- **8×–16×** for tabletop exercises focused on situational
  awareness and persona coordination, not on dispatch latency.
- **32×** for very-high-tempo overview exercises where the
  goal is the arc, not any single decision.

Warp can change mid-exercise (controller envelope; audit-
logged). Be deliberate: changing warp shifts what is being
practised.

## 5. Brief participants

Before starting:
- Confirm every participant is signed in to the exercise
  tenant under the appropriate persona.
- Confirm the persona switcher shows the expected personas.
- Walk through the scenario at a high level (without revealing
  injects unless you intend a fully-briefed exercise).
- Confirm the warp ratio and the expected exercise duration.
- Confirm the learning objectives.

For live-fire and functional, also confirm:
- Which agency systems-of-record are participating.
- The communication channel for out-of-Hub coordination
  (radio frequencies, phone bridges).
- The recall conditions if the exercise must be terminated
  early (e.g., real incident emerges in the same area).

## 6. Run the exercise

During the exercise, the controller's primary tasks:

- **Monitor agent emissions** for fidelity drift; agents
  occasionally produce nonsensical recommendations during
  novel scenarios. Pause and re-seed if needed.
- **Inject** new signals or events at planned moments via the
  controller chat (*"inject a power outage at Khancoban
  substation now"*); each inject is audit-logged with the
  controller's identity.
- **Coordinate** between participating personas — answer
  questions, clarify scenario details, observe decisions.
- **Pause** for hot-debrief moments where you want the room
  to reflect mid-stream.
- **Record** verbal decisions via the *"declared decision"*
  affordance for tabletops where decisions don't otherwise
  produce audit rows.

The controller is **not** a participant. Don't mix controller
and operating-persona actions in the same session.

## 7. Stop the exercise

The *Stop* control archives the exercise; transitions PPRR to
Recovery; freezes the audit trail; closes participant sessions.
This is one-way — once stopped, an exercise cannot be resumed.

Before stopping, confirm:
- Every learning objective is either achieved or explicitly
  marked as not-achieved (with rationale; audit-logged).
- Every open inject is closed (declared either resolved or
  abandoned).
- Every participant has acknowledged the exercise end.

## 8. Debrief

The debrief is the most valuable part of the exercise. The
Hub supports debriefs via the **Replay** workspace at
`/simulations/{tenantId}/replay`:

- Scrub the timeline; pause at decision points; ask
  participants what they saw and why they acted.
- Open the audit timeline alongside; show the rows that record
  each decision.
- Open the agent reasoning traces for key recommendations;
  discuss whether the agent's reasoning matched the
  participant's mental model.
- Compare the actual outcome with the learning objectives.

Capture findings via Observer mode (see
[`observer-mode-guide`](/help/articles/observer-mode-guide))
during the debrief; export an evidence pack for the formal
report.

## 9. Post-exercise

After debrief:
- File the exercise's after-action report (outside the Hub).
- Archive the exercise tenant (it becomes read-only; replay
  remains available indefinitely).
- Update the standing orders and playbooks if the exercise
  surfaced doctrinal gaps; submit changes via the help-corpus
  authoring workflow.
- If the exercise revealed a calibration drift in agent
  recommendations, raise with Risk Manager for re-calibration.

## 10. Common pitfalls

### 10.1 Over-warping
Warp 32× compresses a 24-hour scenario into 45 minutes. The
audit trail is dense; participants miss inflection points. If
the goal is decision-arc practice, prefer 4×–8×.

### 10.2 Mid-exercise persona switching
Participants want to "play" multiple personas. The Hub
disables mid-exercise persona switching for replay fidelity.
Plan ahead: assign each participant a single persona for the
session, or run paired exercises for multi-persona experience.

### 10.3 Cross-tenant leakage
Participants signed in to both an exercise tenant and a
production tenant in different browser windows must be
careful: a published alert from the exercise tenant goes to
exercise channels (Hub-only), not real-world channels, but
verbal coordination over an out-of-Hub channel can leak
intent. Use the *"Exercise"* watermark as a constant reminder.

### 10.4 Insufficient learning objectives
Exercises without explicit learning objectives debrief poorly
— the conversation drifts to whoever's anecdote is most
salient. Capture three to five objectives at creation, even
for short exercises.

### 10.5 No Observer
Exercises without an Observer/Auditor in the room miss most
of the learning. Even a single Observer recording findings
during the exercise dramatically improves debrief quality.
