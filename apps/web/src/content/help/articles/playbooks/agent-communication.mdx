# Playbook: working with the communication agent

This playbook complements the
[Communication agent workspace](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/agent-communication).
It covers how PIOs should consume the agent's draft proposals,
when to accept, when to amend, when to start fresh, how to
handle multilingual handoff, and how the agent should and
should not influence community-facing alert authoring.

The communication agent is a **drafting assistant**, not a
publisher. Every published alert is an authored work of the
PIO and the co-sign chain; the agent's role is to accelerate
the drafting and to ensure plain-language and multilingual
considerations are surfaced early.

## 1. Reading a proposal

Each proposal carries:
- A **subject** — the incident or hazard cluster.
- A **severity recommendation** — Advice / Watch and act /
  Emergency warning / All clear.
- A **confidence band** on the proposal itself.
- **Recommended channels** — based on severity + audience
  reachability.
- **Recommended languages** — based on language pocket
  coverage.
- A **draft headline** + **draft lead instruction** in
  English.
- A **style-guide drawer** with the rules applied and the
  precedent alerts considered.

Read in this order: subject → severity → drivers → headline →
lead instruction → channels → languages. Open the precedent
alerts; their actual headlines are useful calibration for
your own drafting.

## 2. Opening in the Alert Composer

Click *Open in Alert Composer* to launch the Composer with
the agent's draft pre-filled. The Composer's audit trail
records the agent's proposal as the *originating artefact*;
your subsequent edits are recorded as field-level changes.

You are now the author. The agent's draft is a starting
point — discard it freely if it doesn't suit. Doctrinal
authorship rests with the human PIO.

## 3. When to accept the agent's headline

Accept the agent's headline when:
- It contains a clear action verb (*"Move now"*, *"Shelter
  in place"*, *"Evacuate to..."*).
- It names the hazard explicitly.
- It names the geographic specifics (suburbs, routes,
  assembly points).
- The character count fits the strictest channel you've
  selected (typically SMS at 160 characters).

If any of these are missing, amend or rewrite.

## 4. When to amend

Common amendments:
- **Sharpen the action verb** — the agent often produces
  *"You may wish to consider..."*; community-facing alerts
  need imperatives.
- **Add measurable specifics** — *"high ground"* becomes
  *"15 metres above current river level"*.
- **Adjust register** — the agent calibrates to the
  doctrinal style guide; local phrasing conventions
  (e.g., alpine vs coastal terminology) may differ.
- **Add or remove a channel** — the agent's channel
  selection is doctrinally compliant by default but you
  may know the local channel landscape better.

## 5. Multilingual handoff

For non-English language tabs, the Composer pre-fills with
machine-translated drafts. The PIO reviews the machine
draft side-by-side with the English source; the cultural
reviewer co-signs.

Doctrine:
- The PIO is the **author** of every language variant; do
  not approve a machine translation you don't read.
- For languages the PIO doesn't speak, the cultural
  reviewer is the **trusted translator and register
  authority**; their co-sign is non-negotiable.
- Plan multilingual alerts into the operational timeline.
  Cultural reviewers' turnaround is hours, not minutes
  outside extreme emergencies.

For *Emergency warning* with imminent danger, the English
publishes alone if the cultural reviewer cannot turn around
in time; the non-English variants follow when ready, bound
by the same correlation ID.

## 6. Cross-border alerts

For cross-border alerts (envelope spans subdivisions), the
Composer surfaces the cross-border workflow:
- The receiving state's PIO is notified.
- They may *Mirror* (publish the same content into their
  channels) or *Issue paired* (publish their own drafted
  alert with the same correlation ID).
- The agent's proposal accounts for receiving-side audience
  patterns when it knows them; verify with the receiving
  PIO during co-sign.

## 7. Severity escalation and de-escalation

The agent's severity recommendation reflects current
evidence. As the situation evolves:
- **Escalate** by re-issuing as a higher severity (e.g.,
  Watch and act → Emergency warning). The re-issue cites
  the original alert via correlation ID. The community
  receives the new alert; the new alert supersedes the
  original.
- **De-escalate** typically via an *All clear* once the
  threat passes. The agent will propose *All clear* when
  the operating picture warrants; verify with the
  underlying signals before publishing.

Don't ping-pong severity (escalate / de-escalate / escalate
within minutes). Each re-issue erodes community signal-to-
noise.

## 8. Plain-language hygiene

The agent's plain-language rules (CEFR B2; action verbs;
measurable instructions; no jargon) are doctrinal; the
Hub's refusal-hygiene gate flags violations.

Common author traps:
- **Vague time references** — *"shortly"*, *"soon"*, *"in
  the next while"*. Use specific times: *"by 1500 today"*.
- **Conditional language for instructions** — *"You may
  want to..."*; community treats this as advisory and may
  not act. Use imperatives for safety instructions.
- **Jargon** — *"PPRR phase Response"*, *"AIIMS Level 3"*.
  Translate to community language.
- **Multiple actions in one instruction** — *"evacuate
  via Route X to assembly point Y unless your home is
  fire-defendable in which case shelter and seal..."*.
  Split into separate alerts or use a structured format.

## 9. Audit hygiene

Every Composer field edit is audit-logged with hashed values
(the verbatim text is captured at *Publish* time). Co-sign
chain events are audit-logged; channel partial failures are
audit-logged.

Best practice:
- Don't publish a draft you'd be embarrassed to defend in
  a post-event review.
- For amber-confidence alerts, ensure the negative-evidence
  acknowledgements are deliberate, not click-through.
- Keep the *review-by* time honest; an alert that's never
  reviewed is a stale alert.

## 10. Edge cases

### 10.1 Channel partial failure
SMS gateway timeout while web and radio publish. Retry the
failed channel; if persistent, *Mark as best-effort* with
rationale. Don't leave alerts in *Partial* indefinitely.

### 10.2 Concurrent draft on overlapping envelope
Another PIO has a draft open with an overlapping geographic
envelope. Coordinate via cross-sector chat; one PIO
proceeds, the other defers or pairs.

### 10.3 Mid-publication snapshot change
A help-snapshot publishes between Approve and Publish; the
Composer aborts and asks for re-validation. This is rare but
critical — the doctrinal interpretation may have shifted.

### 10.4 Cultural reviewer unreachable
Plan the operational timeline accordingly; English publishes
alone if necessary; non-English follows.

### 10.5 Community-response signal divergence
After publication, the agent monitors community-response
signals (call-centre volume, social sentiment, mobile
movement patterns). If response diverges sharply from
expectation (e.g., expected evacuation movement not
materialising), the agent surfaces a *"Response
divergence"* annotation in the Composer's history pane.
PIO considers a re-issue with sharpened language.
