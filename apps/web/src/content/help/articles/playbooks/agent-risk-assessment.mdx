# Playbook: working with the risk-assessment agent

This playbook complements the
[Risk-assessment agent workspace](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/agent-risk-assessment)
surface reference. It covers how Risk Managers should triage
assessments, when to promote red signals, when to demote
green/amber signals, how to tune thresholds responsibly, and
how non-Risk-Manager personas should consume the agent's
output.

The risk-assessment agent is the **interpreter of the operating
picture**. Where the monitoring agent says *"this is happening"*,
the risk-assessment agent says *"this is what it could mean"*.

## 1. The Risk Manager's daily rhythm

**Shift start (10 minutes):**
- Read the calibration panel. Note the fit timestamp and
  reliability plot shape. If the plot has visibly drifted from
  the diagonal, queue a refit.
- Scan the active assessments queue, top-to-bottom. Form a
  baseline mental model.
- Glance the risk register's composite-score column for any
  score > 15 (medium-high or high); read those assessments
  end-to-end.
- Note any pending promotion / demotion requests from operating
  personas (the workspace surfaces these in a side rail).

**During shift (continuous):**
- Triage new assessments as they arrive (typically every few
  minutes during active events).
- Promote red signals when manual review confirms operational
  significance.
- Demote signals when contradicting evidence emerges.
- Respond to threshold-tuning requests from operations or
  the constitutional steward.

**Shift end (5 minutes):**
- Review the day's promotions and demotions.
- Brief the incoming Risk Manager (verbal handoff is
  appropriate; written handoff via a *"Risk handoff"* note
  affordance for cross-day shifts).
- Confirm calibration is not approaching staleness; if at
  day 50–60, schedule the refit for the next quiet window.

## 2. Promoting a red signal

A red signal (model confidence < 0.55) is hard-suppressed in
operating workspaces by default. Promotion is the Risk
Manager's mechanism for surfacing a red signal to operations
when manual review confirms it warrants action.

Process:
1. Open the red signal in the risk-assessment workspace.
   Read the signal's source, the corroborating signals, and
   the contradicting signals.
2. Cross-check with at least one independent source (a phone
   call to the originating field crew; a peer Risk Manager's
   second opinion).
3. If satisfied, click *Promote* on the assessment card. The
   promotion dialog requires:
   - The promoted band (amber).
   - A rationale paragraph that cites the corroborating
     evidence and addresses any contradicting evidence.
   - An acknowledgement of the doctrinal consequence
     (the signal becomes visible and actionable in operating
     workspaces).
4. The promotion takes effect immediately; affected operators
   see the promoted signal within ~1 second via WebSocket
   push.
5. The promotion is reversible by you within 30 minutes
   (e.g., if you discover an error in your reasoning); after
   30 minutes, reversal requires constitutional steward
   acknowledgement.

Doctrine: be slow to promote. Each promotion erodes the
agent's red-band suppression model; systematic over-promotion
is a calibration problem, not a workflow workaround.

## 3. Demoting an amber or green signal

Demotion is the inverse: lowering an amber to green-suppressed
or a green to amber. Demote when:
- Contradicting evidence emerges that the agent has not
  incorporated.
- The signal's source has been credibly impeached (e.g., a
  field report that the originating BoM station has a known
  fault).
- The operational context has changed in a way the agent
  cannot model (e.g., a wind shift visible from a hilltop).

Process mirrors promotion: rationale required; reversible
within 30 minutes; steward acknowledgement after.

## 4. Tuning thresholds

Thresholds (default 0.55 / 0.85) bound the bands. Tune within
the doctrinal floors and ceilings (0.50–0.60 amber/red;
0.80–0.90 green/amber).

When to consider tuning:
- The reliability plot has drifted such that the calibrated
  probabilities no longer match the empirical hit rates at
  the threshold values. *First* refit calibration; tune only
  if refit doesn't restore alignment.
- The operational tempo has shifted (e.g., a long fire season
  with consistent base rate elevation) and the default
  thresholds are producing too many or too few amber
  recommendations for the doctrinal cost balance.
- A constitutional steward has requested tuning as part of a
  doctrinal review.

Process:
1. Open the threshold tuning panel.
2. Drag the proposed thresholds. The plot updates with the
   proposed bands.
3. Click *Preview impact*. The Hub replays the prior 30 days
   under the proposed thresholds and reports the change in
   volume of amber actions, red suppressions, FP/FN rates.
4. If the impact is acceptable, click *Apply*. The
   constitutional steward acknowledgement prompt appears.
5. The steward acknowledges (or refuses); on acknowledgement,
   the new thresholds take effect across the tenant.
6. Audit-logged with both your identity and the steward's.

Tuning is reversible. The tuning history is on the Governance
Dashboard.

## 5. Non-Risk-Manager personas and risk assessments

If you are not a Risk Manager, you consume the agent's
output via:
- The risk-assessment workspace (read-only assessments;
  red signals not visible).
- The Disaster Map's Recommendations layer (the agent's
  non-suppressed recommendations).
- The Governance Dashboard's KPI panel (composite risk by
  jurisdiction).
- The chat assistant's grounding (cites the agent's
  reasoning when answering risk-related prompts).

When you disagree with an assessment:
- Document your disagreement in the workspace's *Comment*
  field on the assessment card. Comments are audit-logged
  and visible to the Risk Manager.
- For urgent disagreement, use the *Escalate to Risk Manager*
  button. The Risk Manager sees the escalation in their
  workspace banner; expect a response within minutes during
  active operations.

Do **not** circumvent the agent by acting on red signals you
have learned of through out-of-Hub channels. Bring the
information to Risk Manager for promotion review.

## 6. Calibration health

Calibration is the agent's relationship with reality. A
mis-calibrated agent is the most dangerous failure mode —
its recommendations look as confident as ever but their
actual hit rate has drifted.

The reliability plot is the diagnostic. A perfectly calibrated
agent's plot is the diagonal: predicted probability equals
empirical hit rate. Drifts to read:
- *Bowing above the diagonal* — the agent is over-confident;
  acting on its recommendations more aggressively than the
  hit rate supports. Demote the green/amber separator (move
  it down).
- *Bowing below the diagonal* — the agent is under-confident;
  acting on its recommendations less aggressively than the
  hit rate supports. Promote the green/amber separator (move
  it up).
- *Inverted-S shape* — the agent is over-confident on
  extremes and under-confident in the middle; refit calibration.

Refit produces a new isotonic regression fit from the prior 90
days of ground-truth labels. The fit takes ~30 seconds; the
new thresholds are not auto-applied (you must tune
separately).

## 7. Edge cases

### 7.1 Insufficient ground-truth labels
A fresh tenant or one with sparse hazard activity may not
have enough labels for a robust calibration fit. The agent
falls back to the doctrinal defaults (0.55 / 0.85) and the
Confidence Bands gate goes amber. Continue operating
cautiously; collect labels through the operational window.

### 7.2 Adversarial signal injection
If the agent is producing implausible recommendations from
implausible signals (e.g., a "fire" signal from a coastal
underwater observation), suspect adversarial signal
injection or a feed defect. Pause the agent; raise to the
operational SRE; preserve the affected feed's recent rows
for forensics.

### 7.3 Cross-tenant calibration drift
A federated tenant's calibration drift can affect cross-
border recommendations. The federation panel on the
Governance Dashboard surfaces cross-tenant calibration
divergence; coordinate with the affected tenant's Risk
Manager.
