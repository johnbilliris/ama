# Playbook: working with the evacuation agent

This playbook is the operator's guide to the
[Evacuation agent workspace](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/agent-evacuation).
It covers how Operations Officers and Field Coordinators
should triage proposals, when to forward, when to amend, when
to dismiss, how cross-jurisdiction proposals work, and how the
agent should and should not influence your declaration
decisions.

The evacuation agent is the **route-and-capacity calculator**
for the Hub. It does not declare evacuations; it proposes
them. The operator who reads the proposal and signs the
declaration is the doctrinal author of the evacuation, and the
audit trail records that authorship.

## 1. Reading a proposal

Each proposal carries:
- A **geographic envelope** — the area to be moved.
- A **population estimate** — how many people, with the
  estimate's source and uncertainty.
- An **urgency** — *Immediate* / *Within 6 h* / *Within 24 h*.
- A **confidence band** — green / amber / red on the
  recommendation itself.
- **Drivers** — top 3 signals or assessments justifying it.
- **Recommended assembly points and routes** — with capacity
  and current condition.
- **Cross-jurisdictional notes** if the envelope or assembly
  points span borders.

Read in this order: envelope → population → urgency → drivers
→ assembly points → routes → cross-jurisdictional notes. If
any step doesn't make sense, open the reasoning trace.

## 2. Verifying with on-ground reality

Before forwarding to Field Coordinator, verify:
- The **population estimate** against your direct knowledge
  (especially in sparsely populated areas where the
  estimate may be < 50 with high uncertainty).
- The **assembly points** are actually open and adequately
  staffed for the proposed inflow. The agent reads the
  infrastructure layer's status but cannot detect last-minute
  closures.
- The **routes** are actually clear. The agent reads incident
  reports but a freshly-felled tree across the Alpine Way
  may not have been reported yet.

For *Immediate* urgency, time may not allow full verification.
Forward and verify in parallel; trust the agent's data freshness
on a sub-15-minute basis.

## 3. The doctrinal floor: when to forward

Forward to Field Coordinator when:
- The agent's confidence is green or amber (red is
  Risk-Manager-promotion territory).
- The population estimate exceeds your sector's capacity to
  shelter-in-place safely.
- The urgency window is meaningful for the proposed
  operation (don't forward a *Within 24 h* if you can
  organise a more deliberate response in the same window).

Don't forward for the sake of "being seen to act"; the
doctrinal cost of a false-positive evacuation is real
(community fatigue, route congestion, secondary incidents).

## 4. The doctrinal floor: when not to forward

Don't forward when:
- The agent's drivers are stale (> 30 minutes).
- The proposed envelope contradicts a recently-issued *All
  clear* without compelling new evidence.
- A peer Operations Officer has already forwarded an
  overlapping proposal (avoid duplicate declarations).
- The cross-jurisdictional notes show receiving sectors
  unreachable and the urgency permits delay until they
  recover.

In each case, dismiss with rationale. The rationale becomes
training signal for the agent and audit context for reviewers.

## 5. Cross-jurisdiction proposals

Cross-jurisdiction evacuations are heavyweight:
1. The receiving sector's Operations Officer must
   acknowledge before declaration commits.
2. Receiving Police plan route security; receiving Medical
   plan triage capacity; receiving Logistics plan
   accommodation.
3. NEMA acknowledgement is required (Notify or Hand off; see
   [`cross-border-bushfire-nema`](/help/articles/cross-border-bushfire-nema)).
4. Public communication on both sides is co-ordinated
   through the communication agent and the Alert Composer.

Plan for receiving-side latency. Receiving sectors typically
respond within 5 minutes during active operations but expect
longer in degraded conditions.

## 6. Field Coordinator's role

Once forwarded, the proposal lands in the Field Coordinator
workspace as a *pending declaration*. Field Coordinator:
- Verifies on-ground (radio to the affected sector's command
  vehicle or scout team).
- Amends the envelope, assembly points, or routes if
  on-ground reality differs from the agent's proposal.
- Confirms the declaration. The declaration becomes a
  real evacuation envelope visible on the production
  Disaster Map and to the public-information channels.
- Or declines, with rationale. The decline returns to
  Operations Officer for re-consideration.

Field Coordinator's amendments are audit-logged with
delta-from-agent-proposal annotation. Reviewers can later see
how on-ground reality refined the agent's plan.

## 7. Public communication coordination

Evacuation declarations almost always trigger public alerts.
The communication agent picks up the declaration and proposes
alert content; PIO drafts in the Alert Composer; the
declaration's correlation ID binds the alert to the
evacuation.

Sequence the communication carefully:
- For *Immediate* urgency, the alert publishes within minutes
  of the declaration — community needs to act now.
- For *Within 6 h*, sequence the alert with the on-ground
  preparation. Premature alert before assembly points are
  staffed produces community frustration.
- For *Within 24 h*, the alert often goes out as *Watch and
  act* with a planned escalation to *Emergency warning* as
  the urgency window narrows.

## 8. Recall and re-issue

If conditions change such that the evacuation should be
recalled (the wind shifts, the threat passes, a forecast
update reduces the projection):
- Open the active declaration in the Field Coordinator
  workspace.
- Click *Recall*. The recall dialog requires rationale and
  asks for the public-communication co-ordination plan
  (typically an *All clear* alert).
- The recall is audit-logged; the *All clear* alert binds
  to the declaration's correlation ID.

Don't recall casually. A recall followed by a re-issue within
the same operational period erodes community trust.

## 9. Edge cases

### 9.1 Proposal during shift handover
A proposal arriving mid-handover should be acknowledged by
the outgoing operator and explicitly handed off to the
incoming operator with verbal context. Don't let it sit in
limbo.

### 9.2 Receiving side declines
If the receiving sector's Operations Officer declines the
acknowledgement (e.g., capacity is exceeded), the
declaration cannot proceed at the proposed assembly points.
Field Coordinator may amend to alternative assembly points;
otherwise the declaration is deferred.

### 9.3 Mass-casualty cascade
A *prepare-evacuation* recommendation that arrives alongside
a *casualty surge* recommendation from the medical sector
deserves a coordination call between Operations and Medical
before forwarding. The agent doesn't model casualty surge
constraints on evacuation routes.

### 9.4 Vulnerable populations
The agent uses ABS census plus accessibility profile data to
flag vulnerable populations (high concentration of older
Australians, mobility-impaired residents, dependent care
facilities). The proposal annotates these flags; Field
Coordinator should coordinate with Medical for
specialised transport.
