# Screen: Persona switcher

The **persona switcher** is the small but doctrinally critical
control in the top bar that determines which decision-rights
envelope your subsequent actions execute under. Switching persona
is **never** a cosmetic change — it changes what you can read,
what you can act on, what evidence you must reckon with, and what
the audit trail records you as. This article documents the
control, the IdP claim model that drives it, the audit
consequences of every switch, and the constitutional rules that
forbid certain transitions.

For the broader doctrinal framing, see
[`constitution-principle-v`](/help/articles/constitution-principle-v).

## 1. The control

The switcher sits in the top bar to the left of the jurisdiction
filter. It reads, in its collapsed form:

> **Persona: Operations Officer**

…with a small chevron indicating that it is interactive. Click
the chip to expand the popover.

The collapsed chip uses a persona-specific colour band on its left
edge: ocean for operating personas, plum for read-and-plan, slate
for Observer/Auditor and Executive Leader, sand for the front-
line variants, and crimson for Commonwealth.

## 2. The popover

The popover lists every persona in your IdP claim, grouped by
operational class:

- **Operating** — Incident Commander, Operations Officer, Planning
  Officer, Public Information Officer, Logistics Officer, Field
  Coordinator.
- **Read and plan** — Resilience Planner, Risk Manager.
- **Read only** — Observer/Auditor, Executive Leader.
- **Front-line** — Frontline Fire / Police / Medical / SES-Marine.
- **Aggregate** — Cross-sector, Commonwealth.

Each persona row shows:
- The persona name.
- Its envelope summary (one sentence).
- A *"Help on this role"* link that opens the persona's landing
  article.

The currently active persona is highlighted with a check icon.
Click any other persona to switch.

## 3. Single-persona claim

If your IdP claim grants exactly one persona, the switcher is
disabled — its chip is greyed out and clicking opens a banner
*"You hold a single persona. Contact your administrator to
acquire additional roles."*. There is no in-app workaround;
session-scoped persona elevation is forbidden by the Constitution
(see Principle V).

## 4. Default persona

If your IdP claim grants more than one persona, the IdP may
nominate a **default persona** via the `default_persona` claim
attribute. The Hub uses this on sign-in to choose your initial
persona without prompting.

If the IdP does not nominate a default, the persona switcher
appears with no selection on first sign-in; you must choose
explicitly. The choice is audit-logged and the Hub remembers it
for the session (subsequent page loads in the same session
retain the choice; a fresh sign-in re-prompts).

## 5. Switch effects

When you switch persona, the following happens, in order:

1. The Hub validates that the target persona is in your claim
   set. (It will be — the popover only shows claimed personas —
   but the validation is the last-line defence against a stale
   popover state.)
2. An audit row appends with action `actor.persona_change`,
   recording the prior and new personas, the active jurisdiction
   filter, the surface you are on, and the snapshot in force.
3. The Hub re-evaluates the current page against the new
   envelope:
   - If the page's required envelope is incompatible with the
     new persona, the Hub navigates to the new persona's default
     landing surface.
   - Otherwise, the page stays put but its action controls
     re-evaluate (see §6).
4. The persona-specific chrome refreshes — workspace tile sets,
   layer overlays, default jurisdiction filter (if any persona
   constraint applies), default theme accent.
5. The next chat prompt sent to the Help Assistant is grounded
   with the new persona context.

## 6. Action controls re-evaluation

After a switch, every action button on the current page re-
evaluates against the new envelope. The most common transitions:

- Buttons your prior persona could press but your new persona
  cannot: become disabled with an aria-tooltip explaining the
  envelope mismatch (e.g., *"Operations Officer cannot publish
  warnings — switch to Public Information Officer or hand off"*).
- Buttons your new persona can press but your prior could not:
  become enabled.
- Buttons that require co-signing under the new envelope (e.g.,
  amber co-sign requirements differ across personas): show the
  *"Requires second eyes"* annotation.

If you are mid-action (e.g., you have an Alert Composer open with
a draft), switching persona does **not** discard the draft — the
draft is bound to the originating persona; you cannot
*Approve* from the new persona unless it is in the originating
persona's co-sign chain.

## 7. Switching mid-exercise

Inside an exercise tenant, persona switching is disabled while the
exercise clock is running. The Hub presents *"Persona switch
refused: shadow exercise active"*. To switch:
- Pause the exercise (Incident Commander or Operations Officer
  envelope), switch persona, resume.
- Or hand off the exercise controller role to another participant
  before switching.

This is doctrinal: exercise replay must reproduce the same actor
envelope at every step, so mid-exercise switches break the replay
fidelity.

## 8. Switching during a workflow

Some workflows span personas (e.g., a PIO drafts an alert, an IC
co-signs). The Hub does not treat these as switches — they are
co-sign chain events. Each persona acts in their own envelope;
the workflow's correlation ID binds them.

If you are *inside* a co-sign chain (i.e., another persona has
queued an action for your sign-off), switching persona before
signing dismisses the request from your new-persona inbox; it
remains pending in your prior persona's view (which you can
return to). This is so a hand-off via persona switch is
intentional, not accidental.

## 9. Audit consequences

Every switch records:
- Actor identity.
- Prior persona, new persona.
- Active jurisdiction filter (unchanged by the switch itself).
- Active surface (the page you were on).
- Snapshot in force.
- Timestamp.

Switches are visible in the audit timeline as `actor.persona_change`
rows. Filtering the timeline by your identity and this action
verb shows your full persona trajectory across a shift.

## 10. Edge cases

### 10.1 Claim revoked mid-session
If your administrator removes a persona from your claim while you
hold that persona active, the next page load detects the
revocation, switches you to the next-most-privileged remaining
persona (or signs you out if no claim remains), and audit-logs
the revocation event.

### 10.2 Constitutional steward intervention
The constitutional steward may, in rare circumstances, force a
persona switch on an actor (e.g., to remove a compromised
persona from active use). This appears as an external event and
is audit-logged with the steward's identity and rationale.

### 10.3 Same-persona "switch"
Selecting the currently active persona from the popover is a
no-op; the Hub does not audit-log a self-switch.

### 10.4 Front-line persona auto-binding
Front-line personas are auto-bound to their sector's home
jurisdiction at sign-in (see *Persona-constrained filters* in
[`screen-by-screen/multi-jurisdiction-filter`](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/multi-jurisdiction-filter)).
Switching *into* a front-line persona automatically narrows the
filter; switching *out* restores the prior filter.

## 11. Keyboard shortcuts

| Shortcut | Action                                         |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `Alt+P`  | Open the persona switcher popover              |
| `Tab`    | Focus persona rows in document order           |
| `Enter`  | Switch to the focused persona                  |
| `Esc`    | Close popover without switching                |
