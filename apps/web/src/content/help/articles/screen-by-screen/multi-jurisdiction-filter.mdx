# Screen: Multi-jurisdiction filter

The **multi-jurisdiction filter** is the small but doctrinally
significant control in the top bar that scopes every operating
view to the jurisdictions you have selected. It is present on
every persona-scoped surface (Disaster Map, every Workspace,
Sector pages, the Alert Composer, the Governance Dashboard), and
its state is persisted per browser. This article documents the
control, the doctrinal model, and the most common pitfalls.

For the broader doctrinal context (jurisdiction as one axis of
the decision-rights envelope), see
[`constitution-principle-v`](/help/articles/constitution-principle-v).

## 1. The control

The control sits in the top bar to the right of the persona
switcher. It reads, in its collapsed form:

> **Jurisdictions: NSW, VIC + 1 more**

…or, if all claimed jurisdictions are selected:

> **Jurisdictions: All (5)**

…where the trailing number is the count of jurisdictions in your
IdP claim. Click anywhere on the chip to expand the popover.

## 2. The popover

The popover lists every jurisdiction in your IdP claim with a
checkbox. Above the list, three quick-action links:
- **Reset to claim union** — selects every claimed jurisdiction.
- **Clear all** — selects nothing (which produces the *"No data
  for this filter"* state; rarely useful).
- **Invert** — toggles every checkbox.

Below the list, a small persistent reminder:
> *Persisted per browser. To change your claimed jurisdictions,
> contact your administrator.*

The popover closes on `Esc`, on click-outside, or on the popover's
*Apply* button. Selections take effect on *Apply*, not on each
checkbox — this is intentional, to avoid noisy refetch storms when
checking multiple boxes in succession.

## 3. The jurisdiction taxonomy

The Hub's jurisdiction codes follow ISO-3166-2 AU subdivisions:

| Code  | Subdivision                  |
| ----- | ---------------------------- |
| `NSW` | New South Wales              |
| `VIC` | Victoria                     |
| `QLD` | Queensland                   |
| `WA`  | Western Australia            |
| `SA`  | South Australia              |
| `TAS` | Tasmania                     |
| `NT`  | Northern Territory           |
| `ACT` | Australian Capital Territory |

Plus three Hub-specific codes:

| Code  | Scope                                                     |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `CW`  | Commonwealth (NEMA, BoM, Geoscience Australia)            |
| `XB`  | Cross-border coordination cell                            |
| `OS`  | Overseas (Australian assistance to neighbours; Pacific)   |

Sub-jurisdictions (LGAs, fire districts, sector areas) are not
first-class in the filter; they are surfaced as overlays inside
the chosen state/territory.

## 4. Default and persistence

On a fresh device or after clearing browser storage, the filter
defaults to your **claim union** — every jurisdiction your IdP
claim grants. Subsequent edits persist in `localStorage` under
the Hub's origin; they survive across sign-out/sign-in within the
same browser.

Persistence is **per browser, per device**. Switching to your
phone resets to the claim union; switching back to your laptop
restores the laptop's saved selection.

The Hub does **not** synchronise the filter server-side. This is
deliberate — the filter is a UI scoping aid, not a security
control. Doctrinal scoping (what you may *act* on) is enforced
server-side from your IdP claim regardless of the filter.

## 5. How filtering composes

The filter is a **scoping** of your envelope, not an **expansion**.
Concretely:

- **Read scope** — every read query (signal lists, warning lists,
  audit-trail filters, KPI panels) is intersected with the active
  filter set. Out-of-filter artefacts are not returned.
- **Action scope** — every state-changing action validates that
  the action's effect is within the intersection of (claim ∩
  filter). An Operations Officer with claim `[NSW, VIC]` who
  filters down to `NSW` cannot issue a VIC task even though the
  claim grants it; re-broadening the filter restores the
  capability.
- **Audit witness** — the filter state at action time is captured
  in the audit row; this is what reviewers see when reconstructing
  the operator's view.

Filtering down does not reduce the audit trail's contents — it
reduces what the audit *list* shows. Reviewers can always widen
the timeline filter independently.

## 6. Cross-jurisdiction effects

If you initiate an action whose effect crosses your active
filter's jurisdictions (e.g., a warning whose envelope spans
NSW and VIC while your filter is `NSW` only), the Hub interrupts
with a *"Cross-jurisdiction effect"* dialog. The dialog offers:

- **Widen filter and proceed** — adds VIC to the filter and
  commits the action.
- **Narrow effect** — re-draws the action's envelope to the
  intersection of the filter; if the resulting envelope is
  empty, the action is refused.
- **Cancel** — no action.

The choice is audit-logged.

If the action's effect crosses *outside* your claim entirely, the
NEMA acknowledgement workflow engages (see
[`cross-border-bushfire-nema`](/help/articles/cross-border-bushfire-nema)).

## 7. Persona-constrained filters

Some personas have a constrained filter that overrides
persistence:
- **Frontline (Fire / Police / Medical / SES-Marine)** — locked to
  the persona's home jurisdiction at sign-in. The popover is
  read-only.
- **Resilience Planner** — locked to the planner's claimed
  jurisdictions; cannot narrow below claim. (Planning artefacts
  are not safely meaningful at narrower scope.)
- **Observer/Auditor** — defaults to the scrutiny ticket's
  jurisdiction set; can widen with confirmation.

If you cannot edit the filter, the popover explains *why* in a
banner and suggests the appropriate persona switch (`Alt+P`) if
you hold a more flexible persona.

## 8. The *"No data for this filter"* state

If the filter narrows to a state where no signals, warnings, or
artefacts exist, the operating views show a *"No data for this
filter"* placeholder. Most common cause: a fresh device with the
default filter that someone narrowed at a prior session but the
claim set has changed.

Click *Reset to claim union* in the popover to recover.

## 9. Keyboard shortcuts

| Shortcut    | Action                                       |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| `Alt+J`     | Open the jurisdiction filter popover         |
| `Tab`       | Focus checkboxes in document order           |
| `Space`     | Toggle the focused checkbox                  |
| `Enter`     | Apply current selection (close popover)      |
| `Esc`       | Close popover without applying               |
| `Ctrl+R`    | Reset to claim union (within popover)        |
| `Ctrl+I`    | Invert selection (within popover)            |
| `Ctrl+0`    | Clear all (within popover)                    |

## 10. Audit consequences

Filter changes are **not** audit-logged on their own — they are UI
state, not state-changing operations. However, every
state-changing operation captures the filter state at action time,
so an audit row implicitly records which filter was active when
the action was taken.

Persona switches *do* record the filter state at the moment of
switch, so reviewers can reconstruct the filter trajectory across
a shift.

## 11. Edge cases

### 11.1 Claim change mid-session
If your administrator edits your IdP claims while you are signed
in (rare; usually requires re-sign-in), the next page load
re-evaluates your claim. New jurisdictions appear in the popover
unchecked; removed jurisdictions disappear and any previously
selected entries for them are discarded with a banner notice.

### 11.2 Non-canonical codes from federated tenants
A federated tenant may use a non-canonical code (e.g., a custom
sub-region). The Hub maps non-canonical codes into a single
*"Other (federated)"* bucket; the bucket is selectable but
artefacts within it carry their original code in their detail
view.

### 11.3 Filter on Help screens
Help screens (`/help/*`) are not persona-scoped or
jurisdiction-scoped. The filter chip is hidden on those screens.

### 11.4 Filter on Governance Dashboard
The Governance Dashboard's KPI panel is jurisdiction-scoped to
the filter; the constitutional-gates panel is **always** tenant-
scoped (gates apply to the whole tenant). The dashboard makes
this distinction explicit in its panel headers.
