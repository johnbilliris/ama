# Constitutional Principle V — decision-rights envelopes

The AMA Hub is governed by a written **Constitution**. The Constitution
is a small, deliberately readable document that defines the doctrinal
shape of the system: what may be done, by whom, under what evidence,
with what audit obligation. **Principle V** is the principle that
defines **personas as decision-rights envelopes** — the cornerstone of
how the Hub maps Australian-Government and AIIMS doctrine onto a
software product.

This article restates Principle V verbatim, explains it in operator
language, lists the envelope membership for each persona shipped by the
Hub, describes the enforcement points, and documents the audit
consequences of every envelope-touching event.

The Constitution itself lives at
`/specs/_constitution/principles.md`; this article is the operator-
facing companion.

## 1. Principle V — the verbatim text

> **Principle V — Decision Rights are Envelope-Scoped.**
> Every state-changing operation in the Hub is bound to a *decision-rights
> envelope*: the cartesian product of (persona, jurisdiction, surface,
> confidence band, snapshot version). An operation may be invoked only
> by an actor whose envelope at the moment of invocation contains the
> operation's required envelope. The Hub MUST refuse operations that
> fall outside the actor's envelope, MUST audit-log the refusal, and MUST
> NOT permit session-scoped envelope expansion. Envelopes are
> compositional but not transitive: holding two persona claims does not
> grant the union of envelopes unless the IdP issued a third claim
> explicitly representing the union.

That paragraph is the operative law of the Hub. Every other section of
this article is commentary intended to make the law actionable.

## 2. The five envelope axes

### 2.1 Persona

Personas are role claims issued by your IdP. The Hub maps each claim
to a fixed persona enumeration; unmapped claims are inert. The
persona's envelope is published at
`/api/help/articles?kind=persona-envelope` and is also visible at the
top of each persona's landing page in this Help corpus.

A persona envelope lists, for each operation class:

- The set of **actions** the persona may invoke.
- The **co-sign requirements** (e.g., amber co-sign).
- The **upstream evidence** required (e.g., signal age ≤ 60 minutes).
- The **downstream propagation** (e.g., publication channels reached).

### 2.2 Jurisdiction

Jurisdictions narrow the persona envelope. An Operations Officer with
claim `["NSW", "VIC"]` may invoke any Operations Officer action **but
only with effect on NSW or VIC artefacts**. Filtering down to `NSW` in
the UI further narrows the envelope at the moment of action; filtering
back up does **not** retroactively expand any prior action's envelope.

Jurisdictions are not transitive: a claim of `CW` (Commonwealth) does
not imply claims on every state; cross-state action requires either an
explicit multi-state claim or the cross-border (`XB`) claim plus a
NEMA acknowledgement step.

### 2.3 Surface

Some actions are bound to specific surfaces. For example, *Approve
warning* is bound to the **Alert Composer** surface; you cannot
approve a warning from the Disaster Map's pin context menu even if
your persona, jurisdiction, and confidence band are all in scope. The
binding is doctrinal: it forces the operator to be in the workspace
that exposes the full evidence panel for the action.

### 2.4 Confidence band

The amber/red bands further restrict the envelope per the doctrine in
[`amber-confidence-badge`](/help/articles/amber-confidence-badge).
Amber actions require co-signing; red actions are suppressed except
for Risk Manager and read-only personas.

### 2.5 Snapshot version

The Hub's help corpus, doctrinal rules, and threshold configuration
are themselves versioned via **snapshots**. Every action records the
snapshot version in force at action time. A subsequent snapshot
change does not retroactively alter the audit interpretation of a
prior action — the action is read in the context of *its* snapshot.

## 3. Enforcement points

Principle V is enforced at five layers, each independently:

1. **UI** — action buttons are disabled when the envelope rules out
   the action. The disabled state shows an aria-tooltip explaining
   why ("Outside persona envelope; co-signer required; jurisdiction
   not in scope").
2. **Web BFF** — the Next.js API proxy validates the persona /
   jurisdiction headers against the destination route before
   forwarding.
3. **API gateway** — FastAPI dependencies (`require_persona`,
   `require_jurisdiction`, `require_co_sign`, `require_band`,
   `require_snapshot`) compose into a per-route policy.
4. **Domain service** — each service rejects out-of-envelope payloads
   with `EnvelopeViolation`, which surfaces as HTTP 403 with a
   structured `code` field for the UI to render.
5. **Audit appender** — refusals and successful actions both append;
   the appender is the last-line defence, ensuring there is no
   silent path past the rules.

A single layer's failure does not weaken the doctrine; you would have
to subvert all five to take an out-of-envelope action.

## 4. Persona envelope summary

The full envelope catalogue is large; this section is a
human-readable digest. Authoritative details are on each persona's
landing page in `/help/personas/<role>`.

### 4.1 Operating personas

- **Incident Commander** — full operational envelope within claimed
  jurisdictions; cross-border requires NEMA ack; amber co-sign with
  any operating persona.
- **Operations Officer** — task issuance, briefing, decision
  logging; warning approval requires co-sign with Incident Commander.
- **Planning Officer** — IAP build, objectives, planning artefacts;
  no public-channel actions.
- **Public Information Officer** — alert composition; alert
  publication requires co-sign with Incident Commander; cross-border
  requires NEMA ack.
- **Logistics Officer** — resource allocation, requisitions; large
  resource moves require co-sign with Incident Commander.
- **Field Coordinator** — signal capture, status updates,
  evacuation acknowledgement; fully offline-first; no public-channel
  actions.
- **Risk Manager** — confidence-band tuning, calibration review,
  red-band promotion/demotion; tuning requires constitutional
  steward acknowledgement.

### 4.2 Sector personas (front-line variants)

- **Frontline Fire / Police / Medical / SES-Marine** — sector-
  scoped operating views; signal capture and status; no
  cross-sector public-channel actions; co-sign with sector
  Operations Officer for state-changing actions.

### 4.3 Read-and-plan personas

- **Resilience Planner** — pre-event planning, frameworks,
  preparedness gap analysis; no operational actions.
- **Risk Manager** — see Operating section.
- **Observer/Auditor** — full read access including red band; no
  state-changing actions; evidence-pack export.
- **Executive Leader** — Governance Dashboard, KPIs, evidence-pack
  export; no operational actions.

### 4.4 Aggregate personas

- **Cross-sector** — explicit aggregation across sector personas
  for combined sector views; no individual sector's state-changing
  actions.
- **Commonwealth** — federal-level oversight; cross-border
  acknowledgement authority; no state-level operational actions.

## 5. Refusals are first-class

Every refusal records a row containing:

- The actor and persona at refusal time.
- The attempted action and the surface it was attempted from.
- The envelope-axis that failed (persona / jurisdiction / surface /
  band / snapshot).
- The IdP claim set at refusal time.
- The recommended remediation (e.g., *"Hand off to <persona>"*).

The refusal row is itself in the chained audit trail. It is **not**
attempted-but-discarded data; it is constitutionally significant. The
Hub's chat assistant can quote refusal rows when answering questions
about why an operator could not act.

## 6. Compositionality

Two persona claims compose into the **set union** of their permitted
actions, **but only over the intersection of their jurisdictions and
under the more-restrictive co-sign rule**. Concretely, if you hold:

- Operations Officer (NSW, VIC)
- Risk Manager (NSW)

…then the union of actions you may invoke is:

- All Operations Officer actions in NSW and VIC.
- All Risk Manager actions in NSW only.
- Both subject to the more-restrictive co-sign requirements.

Compositionality is **not transitive**: holding Operations Officer in
NSW and Operations Officer in VIC does **not** grant *cross-border*
actions in the (NSW + VIC) jurisdiction — that requires the
cross-border (`XB`) claim or NEMA acknowledgement.

## 7. Session-scoped expansion is forbidden

You cannot, mid-session, request a temporary envelope expansion. The
only paths to a wider envelope are:

- Sign out and re-acquire claims after your administrator updates
  your IdP role assignments.
- Hand off the action to a persona whose envelope already contains
  it.
- Request constitutional steward escalation, which records an
  exception ledger entry visible to all stewards and tenants.

This is intentional. Any short-circuit would invite social-engineering
attacks against the operating personas.

## 8. Practical examples

**Example A.** A NSW Operations Officer attempts to approve a
warning. The Hub disables the *Approve* button on the Alert Composer
because warning approval is bound to Incident Commander, not
Operations Officer. The Operations Officer chooses *Send to Incident
Commander*; the action is forwarded with full evidence intact.

**Example B.** A NSW + VIC Incident Commander attempts to publish a
warning whose effect crosses into QLD because of geographic spread.
The Hub presents a *"Cross-border action requires NEMA
acknowledgement"* dialog; the IC chooses *Notify NEMA and proceed*;
the action commits with both the IC's identity and the NEMA
acknowledgement recorded.

**Example C.** A Public Information Officer drafts an alert in
amber-confidence territory. Approval requires co-sign with Incident
Commander; the PIO selects an IC from the dropdown of currently
signed-in eligible co-signers; the IC's workspace banner shows the
co-sign request; on the IC's approval, the alert publishes.

**Example D.** A Risk Manager attempts to tune the amber threshold
to 0.49 (below the doctrinal floor of 0.50). The Hub refuses and
records the refusal; the Risk Manager corrects the value.
