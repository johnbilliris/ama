# Cross-sector — getting started

The **Cross-sector** persona is the AMA Hub's aggregate
sector-coordination role: where individual sectors (Fire,
Police, Medical, SES, Marine, etc.) operate within their
specialism, the Cross-sector persona reads across them and
co-ordinates joint actions. Cross-sector is the typical
day-of-operation persona for state-level emergency operations
centres operating in a multi-sector event.

This article gets a brand-new Cross-sector controller
productive in fifteen minutes.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Read** every sector's evidence panel.
- **Read** every front-line persona's workspace.
- **Co-ordinate** joint actions that compose into per-sector
  sub-actions.
- **Initiate** cross-sector tasks with sector heads.
- **Read** all confidence bands except red.
- **Engage** in the cross-sector chat as primary coordinator.

You cannot:
- Take individual sectors' internal actions (e.g., RFS strike
  team rotation is reserved for RFS Operations).
- Approve public warnings (PIO + IC).
- Tune confidence-band thresholds.

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in**; you land at the Cross-sector workspace.
2. **Read the per-subdivision rows** showing each sector's
   status as a cell.
3. **Read pending cross-sector actions** awaiting
   coordination.
4. **Read the cross-sector chat** for the last hour to catch
   up on coordination patterns.
5. **Read the audit timeline** filtered to cross-sector
   actions for the last 12 hours.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing Cross-sector controller.

## 3. The cross-sector workspace

The workspace surfaces:
- **One row per affected subdivision** with each sector's
  status as a cell.
- **A column of co-ordinated actions** that compose into
  per-sector sub-actions on approval.
- **A timeline of declarations** from each sector's
  front-line persona.
- **The cross-sector chat** as the right rail.

Co-ordinated actions are the workspace's signature
affordance — composing a single action that fans out into
sector-specific sub-actions on approval.

## 4. Joint task issuance

A joint task example: *"Establish a multi-agency staging
area at the Tumbarumba showground"*. The composition:
- Police: traffic control around the ingress points.
- Fire: appliance staging.
- Medical: triage tent.
- Logistics: fuel and accommodation.

The joint task surfaces in each sector's task inbox; sectors
acknowledge separately; the joint task progresses when all
sectors have acknowledged.

## 5. Cross-sector chat

The cross-sector chat is the primary coordination channel.
Use it for:
- Briefings to multiple sector heads simultaneously.
- Conflict resolution when sectors propose contradictory
  actions.
- Asking questions across sectors.
- Logging coordination decisions (the chat is audit-logged).

Don't use it as primary task issuance — tasks go through the
task affordance for proper structure and tracking.

## 6. Conflict resolution

When sectors propose conflicting actions (e.g., Fire wants
to evacuate via Route A; Police wants to keep Route A clear
for emergency vehicles), the Cross-sector controller chairs
the resolution:
- Engage both sector heads in the chat.
- Read both sectors' evidence.
- Propose a resolution.
- Document the agreed action in the audit trail.
- Both sectors execute.

## 7. Working with the Commonwealth persona

Cross-sector co-ordination that scales beyond the state
engages the Commonwealth persona via the cross-jurisdictional
workflow. See [`cross-border-bushfire-nema`](/help/articles/cross-border-bushfire-nema)
for the playbook.

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Acting within a sector's envelope
Don't try to take a sector's internal actions; you don't
hold their envelope. Engage the sector's Operations Officer.

### 8.2 Joint task fragmentation
A joint task whose sub-actions don't actually require each
other shouldn't be a joint task; issue separate tasks.

### 8.3 Chat as audit substitute
The chat is audit-logged but it isn't structured. Major
decisions go in the Decision Log; the chat is for the
narrative around them.

### 8.4 Cross-sector handover without verbal
A coordination handover at shift change without verbal
context produces silent drift. Talk to your incoming peer.
