# Incident Commander — getting started

The **Incident Commander (IC)** holds AIIMS Controller authority
for an incident or set of related incidents within their claimed
jurisdictions. In the AMA Hub the IC's decision-rights envelope
is the broadest of any operating persona: warning approval,
cross-jurisdiction escalation, evacuation envelope confirmation,
amber co-sign authority for every other operating persona, and
NEMA acknowledgement initiation. With the breadth comes the
audit weight — every IC decision becomes part of the post-event
record.

This article gets a brand-new IC productive in fifteen minutes,
documents the decision-rights envelope in detail, walks through
the typical-shift activity arc, names the most common pitfalls,
and indexes the IC-relevant articles across the help corpus.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Approve** public warnings (final co-sign on the chain;
  PIO drafts, you approve, the alert publishes).
- **Confirm** evacuation envelopes (Field Coordinator declares;
  you confirm cross-jurisdictional and large-population
  envelopes).
- **Co-sign** any operating persona's amber-band action
  (Operations Officer warning, Logistics Officer transfer, PIO
  alert when amber).
- **Initiate** NEMA acknowledgement for cross-jurisdictional
  effects.
- **Escalate** to the constitutional steward for doctrinal
  exceptions.
- **Switch** persona (if your IdP claim grants others) but not
  mid-exercise.

You cannot:
- Bypass the audit trail.
- Tune confidence-band thresholds (Risk Manager's role).
- Author or edit help articles (content authoring is separate).
- Take an action whose effect lies outside your claimed
  jurisdictions without NEMA acknowledgement.

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in** via your IdP. Land at the Disaster Map.
2. **Glance the AU-region banner** — must read *"Hosted in
   Australia"*.
3. **Open the Governance Dashboard** (`g g`) — read the ten
   constitutional gates. Any amber or red? If so, that
   shapes your shift.
4. **Open the Audit timeline** (`g a`) — read the last hour's
   actions. Get a sense of the operational tempo.
5. **Return to the Disaster Map**. Take in the operating
   picture. Note any signals or warnings you don't recognise
   from prior briefings.
6. **Open your inbox** (workspace tile) — pending co-sign
   requests should be cleared first.
7. **Open the Monitoring agent workspace** (`g w`, `m`). Read
   the top three recommendations.
8. **Verbal handover** with the outgoing IC (face-to-face if
   possible; phone otherwise). Confirm in-flight decisions
   and outstanding co-signs.

## 3. Your typical shift arc

The IC's day is shaped by the operating tempo. In quiet
periods, you are reviewing posture and preparing. In active
events, you are co-signing under time pressure.

- **Quiet** — calibration health, exercise design, mutual-aid
  agreement freshness, persona claim audits, evidence-pack
  reviews from prior incidents.
- **Watch and act** — increased monitoring queue depth, more
  Risk Manager promotions to consider, more co-sign requests
  arriving. Pace settles into reading-and-deciding.
- **Emergency warning** — co-sign chain dominates your time.
  Expect interruptions every few minutes. Plan for
  hot-debrief moments to maintain situational awareness.

## 4. Co-signing well

Co-signing is the IC's largest single workload during active
events. Doctrine for co-signing well:

- **Read the originating evidence.** Don't co-sign on faith;
  the audit trail records you as a peer of the originating
  persona, and reviewers will look at what you saw.
- **Verify the negative-evidence acknowledgements**. The
  originating persona has clicked through; you should have a
  view on whether their click-throughs were considered.
- **Check the snapshot in force**. The doctrinal interpretation
  is bound to the snapshot; mid-action snapshot publications
  invalidate drafts.
- **Prefer hand-off to delay**. If you're unable to give a
  decision proper attention, hand off to a peer IC or
  escalate to NEMA — don't sit on it.

## 5. Cross-jurisdictional decisions

Cross-jurisdictional effects (warnings whose envelope spans
borders, resource transfers across borders, evacuations
across borders) trigger the NEMA acknowledgement workflow.
See [`cross-border-bushfire-nema`](/help/articles/cross-border-bushfire-nema)
for the full playbook. Your two choices:

- **Notify NEMA and proceed** — the action commits with the
  Commonwealth notified asynchronously. Use for actions whose
  doctrinal authority clearly rests in your jurisdiction
  (e.g., a state-level evacuation that incidentally crosses a
  border).
- **Hand off to NEMA** — the action queues for a Commonwealth
  controller. Use for actions whose character is genuinely
  federal (e.g., a multi-state resource coordination).

The choice is doctrinal; both are audit-logged.

## 6. Refusals and exceptions

You will refuse actions. Each refusal is a constitutional
artefact:

- The originating persona is told *why* you refused.
- The remediation (hand off, gather more evidence, defer) is
  named.
- The audit row records actor, action, originating evidence,
  refusal reason.

Refuse decisively when the evidence doesn't support the
action; refuse compassionately when the originating persona
is over-reaching from genuine concern. Both refusals deserve
a verbal follow-up.

For doctrinal exceptions — situations the doctrine doesn't
contemplate — escalate to the constitutional steward. The
steward records an *exception ledger* entry visible to all
stewards and tenants; the precedent feeds back into doctrinal
review.

## 7. The chat assistant

The Help Assistant is grounded against this corpus. As IC,
common prompts:
- *"What does the amber co-sign chain look like for a PIO
  alert publication?"*
- *"What is the NEMA acknowledgement window for a
  cross-border resource transfer?"*
- *"What's the audit DLQ remediation playbook?"*
- *"What's the difference between *Notify and proceed* and
  *Hand off to NEMA*?"*

The assistant cites every claim. When the cited article is
amber-confidence (rare for help corpus, but possible if
freshly authored), the assistant prepends a *"verify with
primary source"* caveat.

The assistant does not see live operational data. For *"is
the Khancoban substation up?"* — open the Disaster Map's
infrastructure layer. For *"what is the IC playbook for an
upstream substation outage during evacuation"* — ask the
assistant.

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Co-signing without reading
The audit trail records you as a peer; reviewers will see
what you co-signed and at what tempo. A burst of co-signs
under one minute each invites scrutiny in the post-event
review.

### 8.2 Hand-off as deflection
Hand-off is a doctrinal tool, not an avoidance tool. Don't
hand off to NEMA to avoid making the call; hand off when the
action's character is genuinely federal.

### 8.3 Persona mixing
You may hold multiple persona claims (IC + Operations
Officer is common). Don't switch personas mid-action; the
draft is bound to the originating persona and the receiving
persona has its own envelope.

### 8.4 Skipping the Governance Dashboard
The dashboard is your constitutional posture. A red gate that
you didn't see at shift start can blindside you mid-event.

### 8.5 Calibration drift blindness
Stale calibration silently downgrades every confidence band.
If you don't watch calibration, you act on artefacts whose
band you trust at face value but whose actual hit rate has
drifted.
