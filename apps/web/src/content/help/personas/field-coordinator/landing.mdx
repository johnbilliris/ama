# Field Coordinator — getting started

The **Field Coordinator (FC)** is the AMA Hub's offline-first
field persona: signal capture from on-ground observation,
status updates from forward posts, evacuation envelope
declaration, evacuation acknowledgement reception, and the
durable bridge between the operating picture in the
coordination centre and the reality on the ground. The FC
spends most of their Hub time on tablet or vehicle laptop, in
degraded connectivity, and the Hub is engineered for that.

This article gets a brand-new FC productive in fifteen minutes
and documents the envelope, the offline model, the declaration
workflow, and the most common pitfalls.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Capture signals** from direct observation (fire fronts,
  river levels, road conditions, infrastructure status).
- **Update status** of forward posts (location, capacity,
  resource state).
- **Declare evacuation envelopes** following Operations
  Officer / agent recommendation, or independently from
  on-ground observation requiring upward escalation.
- **Acknowledge** evacuation directives received from
  Operations.
- **Operate offline** with full action capture and replay on
  reconnection.

You cannot:
- Approve public warnings.
- Issue tasks across sectors.
- Allocate resources beyond your declared post.
- Take public-channel actions (PIO's role).

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in** on your field device (tablet or laptop). Land
   at the Field workspace.
2. **Confirm the AU-region banner** and the offline mode
   icon (a small cloud icon: filled = online, empty = offline).
3. **Declare your post** — the geographic location you'll
   operate from. The post defines your reachable-radius and
   your operating picture's local centre.
4. **Read the active envelopes** in your area — current
   warnings, evacuation declarations, agent recommendations
   for prepare-evacuation.
5. **Read your inbox** — any directives from Operations
   awaiting acknowledgement.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing FC. Confirm any
   in-flight observations or queued actions.

## 3. The offline model

The FC workspace registers a service worker. This means:
- Critical actions (signal capture, status update, evacuation
  acknowledgement) work when you have no network.
- Actions queue locally in IndexedDB; the queue persists
  across browser restarts.
- When connectivity returns, the queue replays in capture
  order.
- Each action's audit row records both the **observed time**
  (when you took it) and the **synced time** (when it
  reached the centre).
- Map tiles for your reachable radius are aggressively
  cached; tile freshness shows in the legend.

A persistent banner reads *"Offline — N actions queued"*.
Don't ignore it; a queue depth growing during a long
deployment is a sign your sync window is unrealistic.

## 4. Capturing signals well

A captured signal is the operating picture's input. The
quality of your capture directly affects the agents'
recommendations. Best practice:
- **Be specific** — *"Fire front 50 m west of Khancoban
  pumper, advancing 2 m/s"* not *"fire near Khancoban"*.
- **Stamp time and location** — the workspace defaults to
  *now* and your GPS-derived location; correct as needed
  before submitting.
- **Photograph if safe** — the workspace's camera affordance
  attaches a photo; metadata is preserved for later
  verification.
- **Tag confidence** — your own assessment of how reliable
  your observation is. The agents weight your captures.
- **Submit promptly** — even offline. The capture queues;
  the centre sees it on next sync.

## 5. Declaring evacuation envelopes

Evacuation declarations are heavy actions, even from the
field. Process:
1. Open the *Declare envelope* affordance.
2. Draw the geographic envelope on the map (touch on tablet,
   click-drag on laptop).
3. Specify population estimate (your direct observation;
   the agent's estimate is shown for comparison).
4. Specify urgency (*Immediate* / *Within 6 h* / *Within 24
   h*).
5. Specify recommended assembly points and routes.
6. Submit.

The declaration:
- If pre-approved by Operations Officer (you're acting on a
  forwarded agent recommendation), commits immediately.
- If novel (your on-ground observation requires upward
  escalation), queues for Operations co-sign.

For *Immediate* novel declarations, coordinate verbally with
Operations via radio while the system processes the queue.

## 6. Receiving evacuation acknowledgements

When the public has been warned to evacuate, your post
becomes the receiving point for acknowledgements (in person,
via radio, via the agency app). The Hub's
*acknowledgement reception* affordance lets you log each
acknowledgement quickly:
- Identity (or anonymous count for crowd ackging).
- Method (in-person / radio / app / observed self-evacuation).
- Time.
- Notes.

Acknowledgements feed back into the public-information
agent's audience-response model. They are also the
operational metric for evacuation completeness.

## 7. The reachable radius

The FC's reachable-radius ring on the map is your declared
operational envelope. Stay roughly within it; venture beyond
only with Operations notification (you become harder to
recall and your tile cache may be sparse).

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Letting the offline queue grow
A queue with 100+ actions awaiting sync is a problem — the
centre's operating picture is stale; the agents' models are
stale. Find connectivity and sync as priority work, even if
it means temporary movement away from the front.

### 8.2 Acknowledging without verifying
Acknowledging an evacuation directive without on-ground
verification of the affected population's status is a
constitutional gap. Verify by direct observation before
acknowledging.

### 8.3 Declaring envelope without Operations coordination
For *Immediate* novel declarations, even the verbal
coordination matters. The system supports the action but
the Operations Officer needs to know.

### 8.4 Ignoring the tile staleness stamp
A *Stale tile · last refresh DD/MM* stamp on a region
you're operating in is a signal your geographic awareness
may be incomplete. Refresh when connectivity returns.

### 8.5 Battery and connectivity neglect
Field devices die. Carry a battery pack; coordinate with
the post's logistical support; don't be the FC who lost
the operating picture because the device drained.
