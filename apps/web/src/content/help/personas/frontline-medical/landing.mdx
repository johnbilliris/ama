# Front-line Medical — getting started

The **Front-line Medical** persona is the AMA Hub's medical-
sector front-line role: state ambulance services'
paramedics, advanced-care paramedic teams, retrieval medicine
teams, hospital emergency-department coordinators, and
public-health coordinators during disasters. The persona is
sector-scoped (Medical) and jurisdiction-locked. Medical-
specific responsibilities in disaster response include
triage, casualty transport, hospital surge co-ordination,
public-health monitoring, and the hand-off between
pre-hospital and hospital care.

This article gets a brand-new Front-line Medical operator
productive in fifteen minutes.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Capture signals** — casualty observations (counts,
  acuity), public-health observations (heat-stroke
  presentations, smoke-related respiratory cases),
  hospital surge status.
- **Update status** of your unit, your forward triage
  position, your transport availability.
- **Acknowledge** evacuation directives where Medical
  participation is required.
- **Receive** tasks from your sector's Operations Officer.
- **Read** the Medical-sector workspace's operating picture.
- **Operate offline** with full action capture and replay.

You cannot:
- Approve public warnings.
- Issue cross-sector tasks beyond your unit.
- Take actions outside your sector or jurisdiction.

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in** on your ambulance device or hospital
   coordination terminal. Land at the Medical-sector
   workspace.
2. **Confirm offline mode and tile cache freshness.**
3. **Update unit status** — location, crew composition,
   transport availability.
4. **Read active casualty incidents** and triage points in
   your area.
5. **Read your inbox** — tasks awaiting acknowledgement.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing crew.

## 3. The Medical-sector workspace

The workspace at `/sectors/medical` shows:
- **Casualty layer** — known incidents with casualty counts
  by acuity (P1 / P2 / P3 / P4).
- **Hospital surge layer** — receiving hospitals with
  current ED load and surge capacity.
- **Triage point layer** — declared forward triage points
  with capacity and staffing.
- **Public-health layer** — disease / heat / smoke
  presentations aggregated by area.

## 4. Capturing signals well

Medical-specific specifics:
- Casualties: counts by acuity, mechanism, expected
  transport need.
- Public-health: presentation type, count, geographic
  cluster.
- Hospital surge: current ED load, ICU availability,
  expected surge timeline.

Be specific; the agents and the receiving hospitals depend
on your captures.

## 5. Triage point declaration

When forward triage is needed, your sector's Operations
Officer declares a triage point. Your role:
- Establish the point.
- Update status (capacity, current load, staffing).
- Capture casualty intake metrics.
- Coordinate transport with the resource-allocation agent's
  recommendations.
- Hand off to receiving hospitals via the hospital surge
  coordination affordance.

## 6. Hospital surge co-ordination

Hospitals participating in the disaster response surface
their ED load and surge capacity. The resource-allocation
agent recommends transport destinations based on:
- Current ED load.
- Acuity match (specialist trauma centre vs general).
- Distance from the casualty.
- Receiving hospital's stated surge capacity.

Coordinate verbally with receiving hospitals during high-
volume operations; the Hub captures the formal record but
clinical handover requires voice contact.

## 7. Public-health monitoring

Public-health signals (heat-stroke clusters, smoke-related
respiratory cases, gastrointestinal outbreaks in evacuation
centres) feed into the agents' projections. Capture
specifically:
- Presentation type.
- Count and trend.
- Geographic cluster.
- Demographic skew if observable.

## 8. Working offline

Same model as the other front-line personas: service
worker; queued actions; replay on reconnection; tile cache
for your reachable radius.

## 9. Common pitfalls

### 9.1 Vague casualty counts
*"Several casualties"* doesn't help the agents or the
receiving hospitals. Count and acuity-tag.

### 9.2 Skipping public-health captures
The agents cannot detect a heat-stroke cluster you don't
report. Capture aggregate observations even when no
individual case is critical.

### 9.3 Hospital coordination via Hub only
Clinical handover requires voice. Use the Hub for the
formal record, the radio / phone for the actual
coordination.
