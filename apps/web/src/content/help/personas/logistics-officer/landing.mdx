# Logistics Officer — getting started

The **Logistics Officer (LO)** is the AIIMS Logistics function
in the AMA Hub: resource allocation, mutual-aid co-ordination,
requisitions, accommodation, fuel and maintenance support, and
the dispatch chain that turns Operations Officer's intent into
units actually moving. The LO is the **bottleneck and the
multiplier** — careful staging accelerates response; clumsy
staging starves it.

This article gets a brand-new LO productive in fifteen minutes
and documents the envelope, the dispatch chain, the mutual-aid
agreements model, and the most common pitfalls.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Draft transfers** in the Resource board workspace.
- **Co-sign** export and import sides of resource transfers.
- **Accept or decline** mutual-aid requests from peer
  sectors / jurisdictions.
- **Book** accepted resources into the receiving pool.
- **Recall** units in-transit (with rationale).
- **Manage requisitions** — fuel, food, accommodation,
  maintenance support.
- **Initiate** NEMA acknowledgement for cross-border transfers.
- **Forward** agent recommendations to the Operations Officer
  for envelope expansion.

You cannot:
- Approve public warnings.
- Issue tasks (Operations Officer's role) — but you do
  acknowledge and execute Operations Officer tasks affecting
  resources.
- Tune confidence-band thresholds.

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in**; you land at the Logistics workspace.
2. **Read the resource pool freshness** — last refresh from
   agency systems-of-record. Stale > 5 min is amber.
3. **Read in-flight transfers** — units in transit, pending
   acceptances, pending recall confirmations.
4. **Read inbound requests** — peer mutual-aid requests
   awaiting your decision.
5. **Open the Resource-allocation agent workspace** (`g w`,
   `o`). Note proposed transfers.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing LO. Confirm in-transit
   units' status.

## 3. The dispatch chain

The chain is in [`playbooks/agent-resource-allocation`](/help/articles/playbooks/agent-resource-allocation)
in detail. Your role spans steps 3–5:
- **Draft** the transfer in the Resource board — pick the
  unit(s), confirm origin and destination, set the timing.
- **Originating Operations Officer co-signs** export.
- **Receiving Operations Officer co-signs** import (if
  cross-jurisdictional).
- **You** (or peer LO on receiving side) accept and book.

Each step is audit-logged. The transfer's correlation ID
binds the agent's proposal, every co-sign, your draft, and
the eventual unit movement.

## 4. Mutual-aid agreements

Verify the relevant mutual-aid agreement is active before
drafting. The agreements catalogue at
`/governance/agreements` lists every agreement; the agent's
recommendations cite the relevant agreement; the Resource
board surfaces it in the transfer drafting pane.

Standing terms to verify:
- Cost recovery (who pays for what).
- Command-and-control transfer rules (does the receiving
  side direct the unit, or does the originating side retain
  command?).
- Recall conditions (under what circumstances can the
  originating side recall mid-event?).
- Fuel and maintenance responsibilities.

## 5. Cross-border specifics

Cross-border transfers always involve NEMA acknowledgement.
The receiving Operations Officer co-signs import; the
receiving LO accepts and books.

Coordinate verbally with the receiving LO during high-tempo
transfers — the Hub captures the formal record but radio /
phone is faster for in-transit logistics queries (e.g.,
*"unit at the border, where do you want them?"*).

## 6. Requisitions

Requisitions cover non-unit logistics:
- **Fuel** — for in-event vehicles, helibases, temporary
  generators.
- **Food and accommodation** — for crews on extended
  deployment.
- **Specialist equipment** — beyond standard issue.
- **Maintenance support** — mobile mechanics, parts.

Requisitions go through the Resource board's requisitions
affordance; the workflow is similar to transfers but with
different approval chains depending on cost thresholds.

## 7. International assistance

For OS jurisdiction (Pacific assistance):
- The agent's recommendation cites the MFA diplomatic note.
- Customs and biosecurity require co-ordination with border
  agencies; the Hub doesn't manage these but the LO is the
  liaison point.
- Expect 24-hour-plus turnaround.

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Booking before acceptance complete
A unit booked into the receiving pool before the export
co-sign is complete creates inventory discrepancies. The
Hub's workflow prevents this but verbal-only coordination
sometimes gets ahead of the system; trust the audit trail.

### 8.2 Stale resource pool
A pool > 15 min stale is unreliable. Refresh from systems-of-
record before drafting; the workspace exposes a *Refresh now*
control.

### 8.3 Mutual-aid agreement assumption
Don't assume an agreement is active because it was last week;
agreements occasionally lapse without the catalogue being
refreshed (rare but happens). Verify when in doubt.

### 8.4 Recall as default
A reflexive recall on every operational tempo dip wastes
movement effort. Recall when conditions warrant; otherwise
let units complete their assignment.

### 8.5 Cost-recovery accounting in-event
Cost-recovery accounting is post-event. Don't try to
optimise for it during an active deployment; the audit trail
is the authoritative record.
