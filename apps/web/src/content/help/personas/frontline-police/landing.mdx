# Front-line Police — getting started

The **Front-line Police** persona is the AMA Hub's police-
sector front-line role: NSW Police / Vic Pol / QPS / WA
Police / SAPOL / Tas Pol / NT Police / AFP-ACT field
sergeants, station-level command, and specialist response
units (PORT, STAR, etc.). The persona is sector-scoped
(Police) and jurisdiction-locked. Police-specific
responsibilities in disaster response include traffic and
route security, evacuation route management, public order
in evacuation centres, and welfare checks.

This article gets a brand-new Front-line Police operator
productive in fifteen minutes.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Capture signals** — public-order observations, traffic
  conditions, route security status, welfare-check outcomes.
- **Update status** of your unit and forward position.
- **Acknowledge** evacuation directives received from
  Operations.
- **Receive** tasks issued by your sector's Operations
  Officer.
- **Read** the Police-sector workspace's operating picture.
- **Operate offline** with full action capture and replay.

You cannot:
- Approve public warnings.
- Issue cross-sector tasks beyond your unit.
- Allocate resources beyond your unit.
- Take actions outside your sector or your home jurisdiction.

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in** on your patrol vehicle device or station
   terminal. Land at the Police-sector workspace.
2. **Confirm offline mode and tile cache freshness.**
3. **Update unit status** — location, crew count, capability.
4. **Read active road closures and route security
   directives** in your area.
5. **Read your inbox** — tasks awaiting acknowledgement.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing crew.

## 3. The Police-sector workspace

The Police-sector workspace at `/sectors/police` shows:
- **Road network layer** — current road closures, diversions,
  congestion estimates.
- **Evacuation route layer** — declared routes with security
  responsibilities by unit.
- **Welfare-check layer** — addresses requiring welfare
  checks (vulnerable persons during heatwave, isolated
  properties during bushfire, etc.).
- **Public-order layer** — incident reports relevant to
  public-order management at evacuation centres.

## 4. Capturing signals well

Police-specific specifics:
- Traffic conditions: lane availability, congestion length,
  diversion conditions.
- Route security: barricade status, access-control point
  status.
- Public-order: aggregation size, mood, specific incidents.
- Welfare checks: occupant status, action taken.

Be specific; stamp time and location; the agents weight your
captures.

## 5. Welfare checks

Welfare-check directives arrive in your inbox with the
address, the reason for the check (vulnerable resident
flagged by social services, isolated property during
bushfire, etc.), and any prior interaction history. For each
check:
- Attend the address.
- Make contact (or attempt; document the attempt's
  outcome).
- Capture the welfare outcome (occupant status, condition,
  any action taken).
- Mark complete.

## 6. Route security

When Field Coordinator declares an evacuation envelope, the
declared evacuation routes are surfaced in your workspace.
Route security units are assigned (or you may be tasked).
For each route:
- Establish security at the assigned point.
- Update status (established / patrolling / handed over).
- Capture any incidents (vehicle collisions, civilian
  distress, route blockages).
- Maintain the route's clear-flow status.

## 7. Working offline

Same model as Front-line Fire and Field Coordinator:
service worker; queued actions; replay on reconnection;
tile cache for your reachable radius.

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Slow welfare-check completions
A welfare check that sits open in your inbox without status
updates produces ambiguity. Update at meaningful milestones.

### 8.2 Off-sector queries
Don't try to action evacuation envelope amendments
yourself; the doctrinal authority is Field Coordinator.

### 8.3 Cross-jurisdictional movement without claim
You can't act outside your home jurisdiction; if a directive
takes you into another jurisdiction, the Hub flags it and
you coordinate with the receiving jurisdiction's Police
Operations Officer.
