# Planning Officer — getting started

The **Planning Officer (PO)** is the AIIMS Planning function in
the AMA Hub: building Incident Action Plans (IAPs), maintaining
operational objectives, anticipating future operational needs,
and ensuring the operating picture remains coherent across
shifts. Unlike Operations and IC, the Planning Officer's
envelope is **deliberative** — long horizon, low click-volume,
high-impact.

This article gets a brand-new PO productive in fifteen minutes
and documents the envelope, the IAP workflow, and the
day-of-day rhythms.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Build IAPs** — structured operational plans for an
  upcoming operational period (12-hour shift, 24-hour cycle,
  multi-day campaign).
- **Manage objectives** — the prioritised list of operational
  goals against which the IAP is composed.
- **Forecast** — produce planning artefacts (resource demand
  curves, evacuation capacity projections, communication
  audience projections) that feed Logistics, Field, and PIO.
- **Read all bands** — including amber recommendations from
  every agent (planning artefacts must consider the full
  band; you don't act on amber, you plan around it).
- **Draft warnings** — for IC and PIO co-signing if
  operational tempo requires planning-phase drafting.

You cannot:
- Approve public warnings.
- Issue tasks (Operations Officer's role).
- Allocate resources (Logistics Officer's role).
- Tune confidence-band thresholds (Risk Manager's role).

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in**; you land at the Planning workspace.
2. **Read the current IAP**. Note the operational period and
   the active objectives.
3. **Open the Governance Dashboard** (`g g`). Note any amber
   gates that affect planning (snapshot freshness, calibration,
   localisation).
4. **Read the Risk-assessment workspace**. Note the top
   composite risks; these shape the next IAP.
5. **Read the Monitoring agent's recommendations**. Note any
   `prepare-evacuation` or `escalate-to-risk` items pending.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing PO. Confirm the IAP's
   objectives are still relevant.

## 3. The IAP workflow

The IAP is built in the Planning workspace's IAP composer.
Sections:
- **Operational period** — start and end times.
- **Situation summary** — the current operating picture.
- **Objectives** — priorities for this period.
- **Strategy** — how the operation will pursue the objectives.
- **Resource requirements** — anticipated needs.
- **Communication plan** — internal and public-facing.
- **Safety considerations** — hazards specific to this period.
- **Contingencies** — plans for foreseeable deteriorations.

The IAP is reviewed and adopted by IC at the operational
period transition. The transition itself is audit-logged with
the prior and new IAP IDs.

## 4. Objectives management

Objectives are the IAP's anchor. Good objectives are:
- **Specific** — name the desired state, not the process.
- **Measurable** — you can tell when you've achieved them.
- **Time-bounded** — within the operational period.
- **Owned** — a named persona responsible.

The Hub's objectives affordance enforces these properties via
required fields. The audit trail captures objective creation,
amendment, and resolution.

## 5. Forecasting artefacts

The Planning Officer composes forecasts from the agents'
projections and the operational context:
- **Resource demand curve** — when will we need more
  appliances and from where?
- **Evacuation capacity projection** — given the proposed
  envelopes, what is the receiving-side load?
- **Communication audience projection** — what audience
  segments need addressing across the period?

Forecasts feed into the IAP and into the relevant
operational workspaces.

## 6. Working with agents

The Planning Officer is the agent's most analytical consumer.
Read the reasoning traces; track patterns over operational
periods. Systematic observations (e.g., the risk agent
consistently over-projects bushfire activity in this LGA;
the resource agent under-recommends pre-positioning) feed
into Risk Manager threshold tuning, agent training feedback,
and the standing register revision.

## 7. Cross-jurisdictional planning

For incidents likely to develop into cross-jurisdictional
events, plan the cross-border coordination ahead of need:
- Engage the receiving subdivision's Planning Officer via
  the cross-sector chat.
- Pre-stage NEMA acknowledgement context — the on-call NEMA
  controller appreciates context arriving with the
  acknowledgement request, not after.
- Pre-draft the public-communication coordination plan.

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Optimistic planning
Forecasts that assume best-case conditions often miss the
operational stress test. Build pessimistic-case
contingencies; the audit trail will record both, and
reviewers credit balanced planning.

### 8.2 Stale IAP
An IAP not refreshed at the operational-period transition is
worse than no IAP — Operations and Logistics may act on
stale assumptions. Refresh on every transition; update
mid-period if the operating picture changes materially.

### 8.3 Objective creep
Adding objectives mid-period without removing achieved or
obsoleted ones produces an unfocused IAP. Curate.

### 8.4 Skipping the reasoning traces
The agents' reasoning is the planning-period's training
signal. Read traces deliberately; pattern-recognise across
periods; feed observations to Risk Manager and content
authors.

### 8.5 Detachment from operations
The PO is sometimes physically separated from the active
ops floor. Compensate via the cross-sector chat and via
periodic walks through the workspaces.
