# Public Information Officer — getting started

The **Public Information Officer (PIO)** is the AIIMS Public
Information function in the AMA Hub: the doctrinal author of
every public alert, the steward of community-facing language,
the orchestrator of multilingual communication, and the
clearinghouse for community feedback during operations. The
PIO's audit weight is the highest in the operating-persona set
because public alerts have the longest reach and the most
direct community consequence.

This article gets a brand-new PIO productive in fifteen
minutes and documents the envelope, the alert composition
workflow, the multilingual handling, the cross-border
coordination, and the most common pitfalls.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Draft** public alerts in the Alert Composer.
- **Approve** alerts (with IC co-sign for amber-confidence and
  for *Emergency warning* severity).
- **Mirror or pair** cross-border publications with peer PIOs.
- **Author** community-facing content in non-Composer surfaces
  (workspace banners, agency app push messages, agency social
  posts via the channel adapters).
- **Co-ordinate** cultural reviewers for non-English variants.
- **Revoke** alerts within 5 minutes of publication; later
  requires *All clear* alert.

You cannot:
- Issue tasks to non-communication personas.
- Allocate resources.
- Tune confidence-band thresholds.
- Take state-changing actions outside your jurisdictions
  without the cross-border workflow.

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in**; you land at the Alert Composer's *Drafts*
   inbox.
2. **Read the in-flight drafts**. Note any awaiting your
   sign-off or with cultural-reviewer co-sign pending.
3. **Open the Communication agent workspace**
   (`g w`, `c`). Note proposed alert content the agent has
   prepared.
4. **Open the Disaster Map**. Take in the operating picture;
   note any signal clusters that may produce alert needs.
5. **Glance the Localisation panel** on the Governance
   Dashboard. Note any languages with low coverage or queue
   backlog.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing PIO. Confirm in-flight
   drafts and pending revisions.

## 3. The alert composition workflow

See [`screen-by-screen/alert-composer`](/help/articles/screen-by-screen/alert-composer)
for the surface reference. The PIO's typical sequence:

1. Open Composer (often pre-filled by the communication agent).
2. Verify the originating evidence in the evidence pane.
3. Set severity, hazard class, geographic envelope, temporal
   envelope, classification.
4. Author the English headline and lead instruction with care
   — these are the highest-impact fields.
5. Author the detail body with structured sections.
6. Add languages as needed; review the machine drafts;
   request cultural-reviewer co-sign.
7. Verify channel selection.
8. Acknowledge the negative-evidence cards.
9. Initiate co-sign chain.
10. On final co-sign, publish.
11. Monitor publication state; retry partial failures.

## 4. Multilingual handling

See [`multilingual-authoring`](/help/articles/multilingual-authoring)
for the multilingual model. The PIO's role:

- Author the English first; it is the doctrinal source.
- Add languages from the catalogue based on the affected
  audience's language pockets.
- Review every machine draft before requesting cultural
  co-sign — a draft you don't read is one you can't
  defend.
- Plan the operational timeline around cultural-reviewer
  turnaround. For *Emergency warning*, English publishes
  alone if necessary; non-English follows.
- For First Nations languages, the cultural authority's
  serial review is the doctrinal floor; do not parallelise.

## 5. Cross-border coordination

For alerts whose envelope crosses subdivision boundaries:
- The cross-border workflow engages on Composer publish.
- The receiving state's PIO is notified; they may *Mirror* or
  *Issue paired*.
- NEMA acknowledgement is recorded.
- Both alerts publish simultaneously when receiving PIO
  co-signs.

If receiving PIO doesn't co-sign within the configured
window (default 5 min), English publishes alone with an
annotation. Don't wait indefinitely for the receiving state;
public-safety doctrine privileges acting on your evidence.

## 6. Revoke vs *All clear*

- **Revoke** within 5 minutes of publication if the alert was
  in error (wrong envelope, wrong severity, premature
  publication). Audit-logged with rationale.
- **All clear** alert when the threat passes. Cites the
  original via correlation ID; supersedes the original in
  community channels.

Don't ping-pong: revoke followed by re-publish followed by
revoke erodes community trust. Slow down; verify; publish
once.

## 7. The community-feedback loop

After publication, the communication agent monitors
community-response signals (call-centre volume, social
sentiment, mobile movement patterns). Sharp divergence from
expectation surfaces in the Composer's history pane. The PIO
considers re-issue with sharpened language.

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Vague action verbs
*"You may want to consider moving"* gets ignored. *"Move
now to high ground above 15 metres"* gets acted on.

### 8.2 Multilingual machine-translation rubber-stamping
Approving a machine translation you can't read is a
constitutional gap. Either the cultural reviewer reads it,
or it doesn't publish.

### 8.3 Publishing under amber co-sign without IC
For amber-confidence alerts at *Emergency warning* severity,
IC's co-sign is non-negotiable. If IC is unavailable, hand
off to a peer IC or escalate; don't bypass.

### 8.4 Channel partial as acceptable
A partial publication has uneven community reach. Retry
aggressively; if persistent, *Mark as best-effort* with
rationale and a planned re-publish window when the channel
recovers.

### 8.5 Stale review-by
An alert with a passed *review-by* time is a stale alert.
Re-examine and either re-issue, revise, or all-clear; don't
let the community wonder.
