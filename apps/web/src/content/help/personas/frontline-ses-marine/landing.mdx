# Front-line SES / Marine — getting started

The **Front-line SES / Marine** persona is the AMA Hub's
combined State Emergency Service and marine-rescue front-line
role: NSW SES / VICSES / QFES SES / DFES SES / SA SES / TasFS
SES / NTPFES SES / ACT SES specialist units (flood boat,
swift water, vertical rescue, storm response), and the
state and Commonwealth marine-rescue services (Marine
Rescue NSW, AMSA, Coastguard, etc.). The persona is sector-
scoped (SES-Marine) and jurisdiction-locked. SES-Marine
specialisation includes flood-water operations, storm
damage assessment and remediation, vertical rescue,
swift-water rescue, missing-person searches, and marine
search and rescue.

This article gets a brand-new Front-line SES / Marine
operator productive in fifteen minutes.

## 1. Your envelope at a glance

You can:
- **Capture signals** — flood observations (river levels,
  inundation extent), storm damage observations, marine
  conditions, missing-person reports, search progress.
- **Update status** of your unit, your forward post, your
  vessel.
- **Acknowledge** evacuation directives where SES /
  Marine participation is required.
- **Receive** tasks from your sector's Operations Officer.
- **Read** the SES-Marine sector workspace.
- **Operate offline** with full action capture and replay.

You cannot:
- Approve public warnings.
- Issue cross-sector tasks beyond your unit.
- Take actions outside your sector or jurisdiction.

## 2. First fifteen minutes on shift

1. **Sign in** on your unit device or vessel terminal. Land
   at the SES-Marine sector workspace.
2. **Confirm offline mode and tile cache freshness.**
3. **Update unit status** — location, crew composition,
   capability (flood boat / swift water / vertical /
   marine).
4. **Read active flood / storm / marine incidents** in your
   area.
5. **Read your inbox** — tasks awaiting acknowledgement.
6. **Verbal handover** with outgoing crew.

## 3. The SES-Marine sector workspace

The workspace at `/sectors/ses-marine` shows:
- **Flood layer** — gauge levels, inundation extent,
  projected peaks.
- **Storm damage layer** — reported damage with severity
  and access status.
- **Marine layer** — vessels in distress, sea state,
  marine SAR cases.
- **Search layer** — missing-person searches with search
  pattern overlays and team assignments.
- **Vertical-rescue layer** — height-rescue cases.

## 4. Capturing signals well

SES-Marine specifics:
- Flood: gauge reading, location, time; inundation extent
  by suburb / property; rate of rise/fall.
- Storm damage: location, type (tree / structural /
  electrical), access status (passable / blocked).
- Marine: vessel description, position, persons on board,
  nature of distress.
- Search: search pattern coverage, team progress, found /
  not-found outcomes.

Be specific; include rate-of-change observations for
floods and storms.

## 5. Search co-ordination

Missing-person searches are co-ordinated through the
sector workspace's search affordance:
- Receive the search task with the missing person's last
  known location, search pattern assigned, team
  composition.
- Update progress against the search pattern (segments
  searched, segments remaining).
- Capture findings (objects observed, witness reports,
  found-person outcomes).
- Hand off to receiving sectors (Police if criminal
  context; Medical if found-person needs care).

## 6. Marine SAR

For marine rescue cases, the Hub coordinates with AMSA
(Australian Maritime Safety Authority) for cases in the
SAR region. Your role:
- Capture the distress observation.
- Update unit status as you respond.
- Coordinate with AMSA via the federation channel for
  joint operations.
- Capture found / not-found outcomes; for found cases,
  hand off to Medical.

## 7. Working offline

Same model as the other front-line personas. Marine
operations especially benefit from offline capability —
mobile coverage offshore is unreliable.

## 8. Common pitfalls

### 8.1 Vague flood-rate observations
*"River is rising"* misses the operational signal;
*"Murray gauge at Albury reading 5.42 m, +3 cm in last
30 min"* informs the projection.

### 8.2 Search-pattern non-completion reporting
A search segment partially searched then abandoned without
explicit non-completion reporting is a coordination
hazard. Mark explicitly.

### 8.3 Marine federation handshake delay
For marine SAR cases involving AMSA, a stale federation
handshake delays coordination. Watch the federation
panel; engage AMSA via radio if the Hub is slow.

### 8.4 Specialist capability mis-tagging
A "flood boat" unit dispatched to a storm-damage tasking
is wasted; tag your unit's capability accurately so the
resource agent's recommendations are sound.
