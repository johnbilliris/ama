PART A: PRELIMINARY 
1. 
Authority and consultation 
This Ministerial Charter is issued by the Minister for Police, Fire and Emergency Management (Minister), 
in accordance with section 73B of the Fire Service Act 1979 (Act) and in consultation with the State Fire 
Commission (Commission) and the Treasurer as Stakeholder Minister. 
2. 
Commencement, circulation and continuation 
This Ministerial Charter comes into effect on the date of signing by the Minister. On that day, electronic 
copies will be provided to the Treasurer and the Commission. 
This Ministerial Charter will remain in force while the legislative requirement for its existence remains 
and/or until it is amended, revoked or substituted. 
3. 
Purpose 
The purpose of the Ministerial Charter is to specify the Minister’s broad policy expectations in the 
context of the legislated functions and powers of the Commission, across areas including but not 
limited to principal objectives, strategic direction, scope of activities, performance and financial 
considerations. 
4. 
Limitations 
While the Ministerial Charter may limit the Commission’s functions and powers, and the performance 
and exercise of those functions and powers, it may not extend the Commission’s functions and powers 
nor prevent the Commission from performing a function it is required to perform or comply with under 
the Act or any other legislation. 
5. 
Requirement for compliance 
The Commission must ensure that its business and affairs are conducted in a manner consistent with 
this Ministerial Charter. 
 
PART B: PRINCIPAL OBJECTIVES AND STRATEGIC DIRECTION 
1. Principal objectives 
The Minister expects the Commission to adhere to principal objectives, which are to: 
• 
perform its statutory functions and powers with due diligence and care, in accordance with the 
Act, this Ministerial Charter and any written directions provided by me, as responsible Minister 
• 
effectively control and oversee the financial and non-financial operations of the Tasmania Fire 
Service (TFS) with respect to preventing and extinguishing fires and protecting life and property 
from fire 
• 
oversee and monitor performance against functions and powers delegated to the Fire and 
Emergency Services Commissioner (TFS) 
• 
operate in accordance with the principles and practices of good corporate governance and 
financial management, transparency, accountability, professionalism and evidence-led decision- 
making 
• 
demonstrate compliance with relevant policy and strategic imperatives of the Tasmanian 
Government and the Department of Police, Fire and Emergency Management and all relevant 
federal and state legislation, regulations and by-laws, including the Fire Service Act 1979,

Emergency Management Act 2006, Work Health and Safety Act 2012, State Service Act 2000, State

2. 
Strategic direction 
The Minister expects the Commission to adopt a strategic approach while meeting its statutory 
obligations, fulfilling its functions, exercising its powers, managing its financial and business affairs and 
controlling the operations of TFS, specifically to actively pursue new and existing opportunities to 
demonstrate: 
• 
merit-based employment practices and opportunities for employees and volunteers that 
encourage and embed TFS’ organisational values and the principles of equality, diversity, 
inclusion, transparency, fairness, and integrity 
• 
maintenance and continuous improvement of work-safe practices and systems to encourage 
and ensure greater safety and wellbeing for all workers 
• 
safe, reliable, responsive and timely service to the Tasmanian community in relation to 
preventing and extinguishing fires and protecting life and property from fire and other hazards, 
supported by the adoption of and/or alignment with national fire service standards and 
practices wherever possible 
• 
effective, contemporary standards of corporate governance and strong financial management 
• 
continuous improvement in business practice and operations, including through the 
establishment of realistic and measurable Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) that align with 
other Australian fire and emergency services 
• 
closer strategic alignment and greater collaboration between TFS and SES 
• 
active participation in and contribution to national industry forums, including through 
membership of AFAC: the Australian and New Zealand National Council for fire and emergency 
services 
• 
a child-safe organisational culture that demonstrates zero tolerance for all forms of child abuse, 
irrespective of when or where the abuse may have occurred or could potentially occur 
 
PART C: CORE BUSINESS AND PERFORMANCE 
1. 
Scope of activities 
The Minister expects the core business of the Commission to focus on exercising its functions and 
powers in relation to preventing and extinguishing fires and protecting life and property from fire. This 
includes further and extensive work in preparedness and prevention of fire in the landscape through 
the Fuel Reduction Program which has proven effective in risk reduction. 
The Minister also acknowledges and expects that the Commission will, wherever possible and within 
the context of performing its statutory functions, consider and accommodate the additional emergency 
management response and support roles and responsibilities assigned to TFS through the Emergency 
Management Act 2006 and further detailed in the Tasmanian Emergency Management Arrangements 
(Issue 2). 
 
Considering the potential future amendment or replacement of the Act, the Minister expects the 
Commission to provide constructive feedback and specialist input to the stakeholder consultation 
process to inform the appropriate execution and transfer of relevant functions and powers, if and when 
required.

The Minister expects the TFS and SES to work closely together as well as actively pursue opportunities 
in resource-sharing, efficiencies and synergies to grow capability as a combined organisation, to the 
benefit of the Tasmanian people. This is critical in the preparedness for an increase in the severity and 
number of natural disasters. 
As a contemporary organisation, the Commission is to actively monitor emerging technologies and 
foster increased use of such technology to enhance operations, both internally (eg equipment, 
employee tools and systems) and externally (eg client-facing products and multi-hazard intelligence 
and communications) 
2. 
Performance targets and indicators 
The Minister expects the Commission to develop KPIs to measure and monitor its performance and 
include those KPIs and annual outcomes recorded against those KPIs in the Commission’s Corporate 
Plan. Those KPIs are to align with financial management, customer service and operational response 
standards across the Commission and TFS’ scope of activities and statutory functions and powers. 
The Minister also expects the Commission to engage the services of suitably qualified individuals to 
conduct independent reviews of the Commission’s performance every three years against the 
requirements of the Act, this Ministerial Charter, its Corporate Charter and Corporate Plan. 
3. 
Financial management policy 
The Minister expects the Commission to review and adjust as necessary the adequacy of its capital 
structure, anticipated income and forecast expenditure, and include that information in its Corporate 
Plan. The Minister also expects the Commission to approve and monitor all major capital investment 
programs and projects in the Corporate Plan. All disposals of assets and undertakings are to be carried 
out in accordance with the Act. 
4. 
Borrowing policy 
The Minister expects the Commission to avoid wherever possible or, where unavoidable, minimise its 
external borrowing requirements. This is expected to be achieved by using internally-generated funds 
as working capital and for capital expenditure. If borrowing is unavoidable, the Minister expects to be 
formally briefed on the Commission’s financial position and intention to borrow before any monies are 
borrowed. If necessary, the Commission is expected to minimise the overall cost of borrowing, debt 
and asset management by seeking expert and informed advice on the most appropriate, cost-effective 
option, including seeking and obtaining advice from the Treasurer, as Stakeholder Minister. 
5. 
Distribution policy 
The Commission is not required to make financial returns under the Act. 
 
6. 
Pricing policy 
When setting fees and charges, the Minister expects the Commission to be cognisant of all economic 
influences and factors that are likely to impact the ability of customers to pay for the services provided 
or used. 
7. 
Employment and conditions of employment policy 
The Minister expects the Commission to adopt contemporary human resource management and 
industrial relations standards and applicable Tasmanian Government policies relevant to employment 
and employment conditions, including policies relating to anti-discrimination and bullying, 
redundancy, work health and safety, equality, diversity and inclusion, induction, employee screening

PART D: OTHER MATTERS 
1. 
Extension of operations 
The Minister expects to be consulted if the Commission intends to and before it establishes a business 
presence outside Tasmania. The Commission must seek the Minister’s approval before establishing 
any subsidiaries or joint ventures. 
2. 
Ministerial briefings, information and consultation 
The Minister expects all briefings, reports and any information requested to be accurate, complete and 
provided in a timely manner. 
The Minister expects to be consulted and briefed by the Commission on matters of policy and prior to 
the release of all public announcements involving significant policy or operational issues or changes. 
Where it is practical to do so, the Minister will consult with the Commission through the Fire and 
Emergency Services Commissioner and/or Chairperson prior to the release of any public 
announcement with significant operational or policy implications.
